Presentation Prep Sheet —

COMM 260 · Component I · 5–7 minutes plus questions · 15%

There are no slides. You put your live landing page on the screen and walk us through it. This sheet is how you prepare — fill it in, then practise from it. Don't read from it on the day.

Before the day

Your run order

Fill in what you'll say at each stop. Keep it to a line or two — this is a map, not a script.

# Stop What you'll say Time
1 Who you are as a producer ~30 s
2 The identity — logo, colour, type ~45 s
3 Photography ~1 min
4 Audio (play a section) ~1 min
5 Video (play it) ~1.5 min
6 How the page holds it together ~1 min

Total should land between 5 and 7 minutes. Playing the video eats a third of your time — plan around that, don't discover it live.

The part that's actually graded

You are not scored on delivery. Not eye contact, not pacing, not nerves. You are scored on whether you can account for your decisions.

For every stop above, be ready with:

What I chose: ____________________

What I rejected: ____________________

Why mine was better for this brand: ____________________

Prepare these four defences

Write these out properly. These are the decisions you're most likely to be asked about.

1. A photograph

Pick one image. Why that framing, that exposure, that moment? What would the obvious alternative have been, and what would it have cost you?

2. An audio edit decision

Something you cut, or something you deliberately held. Why?

3. A video choice

Where you departed from your plan, or a cut you made. What changed and why?

4. Linear or non-linear

Which structure you chose for the page, what it makes possible, and what it costs you. Both halves.

The question you will be asked

"Why not the other way?"

Asked about something you did not choose to talk about. That's deliberate — it's the difference between reasoning you hold and reasoning you rehearsed.

You can't prepare for every version of it. What you can do is know, for each piece of work, what the alternative was. If you never considered an alternative, that's the thing to fix before the day.

"It looked better" is not an answer. Neither is "that's just my style." Both are preferences. A defence names what the choice does for the work.

If a question exposes something you got wrong

Say so. "You're right — that doesn't hold, because…" scores higher than defending a weak position. Conceding accurately is on the rubric under Exceeds.
