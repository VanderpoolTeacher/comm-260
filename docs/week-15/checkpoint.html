What a complete answer looks like

The structural justification is the part students under-prepare. Here is one that would be marked Meets:

My page is linear. It reads top to bottom: positioning statement, then the photography set, then the audio, then the video. Nothing is reachable except by scrolling past what comes before it.

That was a decision, and here is what it buys. The statement says the work is about how much of a maker's day is not making. Every piece after it is read through that sentence — the photographs of an empty bench mean something different once you have read it, and I wanted that. A visitor who arrives at the video first would see a man locking a door and nothing else.

And here is what it cost. If someone stops after the photographs, my audio and video did not happen. I have no way to promote the video for a visitor who came specifically to see whether I can shoot video — which, for the arts organisations I say I work for, is quite likely the actual question they arrived with. A non-linear page would have served that visitor better and would have cost me the framing.

I chose the framing. With this set, the argument only works in one order.

What that demonstrates: the structure is identified rather than assumed, the gain is tied to this material, and the cost is one the student would rather not have paid — it names a specific visitor who is badly served. And the final line engages with the rejected alternative rather than praising the choice.

To reach Exceeds, defend it harder against the alternative. This is one step away: "the strongest case for non-linear is that a hiring decision is made in ninety seconds and mine assumes four minutes of patience — I think the framing is worth it at this scale, and I would not make the same call with twice the work on the page."

What does not count

  • A page that is not published. "It works on my machine" is not published, and this is checked first.
  • The removal test passed by assertion. You have not run it. It gets run in the room.
  • Structure justified with gains only. Outcome 15.2 is both its gains and its costs, and week 10 said the same thing.
  • A positioning statement that drifts into a reflection. Feelings about the work rather than who it is for and what conventions it follows. This is the only assessment of LO 10 in the course.
  • Conventions claimed that the work does not follow. Worse than claiming fewer.
  • Crew log signatures missing or duplicated. Week 14 was the last chance to fix this. Here it is simply scored.
  • "It looked better." Week 1's failure, in the final week.
  • A rehearsed tour of the page. Outcome 15.4 is a defence.

What the questions are for

The prepared part of your presentation shows what you decided to tell me. The questions show whether the decisions were real.

You will be asked about Because
A specific choice in one image Whether week 1's naming survived to week 15
Why that structure, and what it cost Outcome 15.2, and you will have under-rehearsed the cost
What breaks if a medium is removed Outcome 15.1, verified live rather than claimed
A departure from your week 14 plan Whether outcome 14.2's honesty holds under questioning
What you would change with another week Whether you can still see your work, or are only finished with it

Use the glossary

This week: Landing page, Positioning statement, Removal test, Integrated, Assembled, Linear, Non-linear, Critique, Presentation, Crew log, Disclosure log, Checkpoint, Course summative.

Two bars that decide marks here. Landing page is barred from website, site, portfolio, and homepage — the page is the integrated piece, not a container for pieces. And positioning statement is barred from artist statement, reflection, rationale, and about page, because each of those is a different document and the different document is what gets handed in.

Rubric

Components E–H are scored on the project rubric; component I on the presentation rubric. The competencies they evidence:

LO 7 — Integration

Integrate multiple media forms (photography, video, audio) into cohesive digital productions.

Level
Exceeds The division of labour is deliberate, and the analysis says specifically what would break
Meets Each medium carries something the others cannot. Removing any one breaks the piece
Approaching All media present, but removing one changes nothing — assembled, not integrated
Not yet Two media saying the same thing, or a third that is decorative

LO 6 — Linear and non-linear

Differentiate between linear and non-linear storytelling methods and apply them appropriately in media projects.

Level
Exceeds Defends the chosen structure against the rejected alternative on its own terms
Meets Distinguishes the two, structures material both ways, and names a genuine cost for each
Approaching Names gains only — or names a "cost" that is a gain restated
Not yet Conflates non-linear with interactive, or produces the linear version with links added

LO 10 — Original professional content

Produce original digital media content suitable for a variety of professional career paths and contexts.

Level
Exceeds The work visibly follows the conventions the positioning claims
Meets Work is original, and positioning names a specific professional context and the conventions it follows
Approaching Original, but the context is generic — "content for people"
Not yet Not original, or no stated context

LO 1 — Aesthetic and narrative principles

Apply aesthetic and narrative principles to create compelling digital media projects.

Level
Exceeds Names the choice, what it accomplishes, and what the alternative would have cost
Meets Names the choice in course vocabulary and says what it accomplishes
Approaching Describes the work accurately but does not name the choice — or names the technique without saying what it does
Not yet States a preference. "It looked better."

LO 8 — Critical evaluation

Critically evaluate media projects for technical quality, aesthetic design, and narrative effectiveness.

Level
Exceeds Distinguishes preference from what the work does not achieve, and names what was lost
Meets All three dimensions, one specific and actionable point each
Approaching Warm, general and true — or three dimensions all making the same observation
Not yet Praise. Not actionable, satisfies no dimension

LO 1 is assessed here for the last time, and largely by the questions — it is the outcome week 1 opened with, and the presentation is where we find out whether "it looked better" was ever replaced with a reason.

The same four levels apply to every assignment in this course. Full descriptors and the whole map of which assignment evidences what: competency rubric.
