Week 15 · Phase 4 — Publish and defend — Lesson

COMM 260 · Introduction to Digital Media Production

Read this before the phase begins. About 20 minutes.

Six things before you arrive:

  1. Components B, C and D final and in hand. This week integrates them. It is not when they get made.
  2. Run the removal test on your own page, before you bring it. Delete each medium, and write what broke.
  3. Draft the positioning statement, 300–500 words.
  4. Complete the presentation prep sheet — the one you were given in week 12.
  5. Crew log signed by all three producers.
  6. Disclosure log complete.

1 · This week integrates. It does not produce.

Your photography came out of week 13. Your audio came out of week 13. Your video came out of week 14.

If you arrive here still making media, you have lost the week — because the work of this phase is putting three finished things together, publishing the result, and defending it out loud.

Nothing in this phase is new. Outcome 15.1 is week 11's outcome 11.2 at full scale. Outcome 15.2 is week 10. The positioning statement is week 12's sentence, evidenced. The critique is week 4, for the fourth time.

2 · The removal test, on your own page

Outcome 15.1 is integrate three media into a landing page that passes the removal test.

That is the same sentence as week 11's outcome 11.2. Deliberately. The difference is scale and that your own brand is attached — and that it is now worth 20% of the project instead of 15% of nothing.

Delete the photography. Delete the audio. Delete the video. Each time, play or read what is left and write down what broke.

Students who passed week 11 by luck rather than by diagnosis meet it again here, and this time there is no revision pass afterwards.

The failure is unchanged from week 11

Two media saying the same thing. A positioning statement that describes the video. An audio piece narrating what the photographs show. Both present, neither necessary.

And the second one: the decorative third. A still used as a header image. Present because a page needs a header, carrying nothing.

Run the test properly and write down the real answer. A page that fails with a correct diagnosis is worth more than a page that claims to pass and was never tested — that is in the competency rubric, and it has been true since week 11.

3 · Your page is a structural decision, whether or not you made it

Outcome 15.2 is justify your linear or non-linear structure with both its gains and its costs. That is week 10, applied to the biggest thing you have built.

Ask one question of your page:

Does anything on it assume something else was seen first?

| If yes | You built something linear and put it on a web page | | If no | You built something non-linear |

Neither is wrong. Not noticing is.

And then the half you will forget

You will name the gain automatically. You must name the cost, and week 10 established that students supply gains reflexively and have to be asked for the other half every single time.

Not a cost A cost
"You lose control, which lets the visitor choose." "Someone can land on my video and never see the positioning statement that explains what any of it is for."
"It's linear, so it's more focused." "If they stop after the photographs, the audio and video did not happen."

If your stated cost sounds like a feature, it is not a cost.

4 · A positioning statement is not an artist statement

Outcome 15.3. 300–500 words. This is the assessment for LO 10 — original professional content — and it is the only one.

It names who the work is for and what conventions you follow. It is week 12's "I make ___ for ___", evidenced at length.

The glossary bars artist statement, reflection, rationale, and about page — and the bar is not pedantry. Each of those words describes a different document, and the different document is what students write.

A positioning statement says A reflection says
Who the work is for How you felt making it
What conventions the work follows What you learned
Where it sits professionally What you would do differently

Nothing about your learning journey belongs in it. It is a professional document, written for someone deciding whether to hire you.

And the conventions have to be visible in the work. A statement claiming conventions your media does not follow is worse than one claiming fewer — because now the page contradicts its own account of itself.

5 · Prepare a defence, not a walkthrough

Outcome 15.4 is defend your decisions live, including ones you did not prepare.

A rehearsed tour of the page does not satisfy it. You will be asked at least two questions you did not anticipate, and that is not a hazard of the format — it is the assessment.

The prepared part of a presentation shows what you decided to tell me. The questions show whether the decisions were real.

What you will be asked about

Because
A specific choice in one image Whether week 1's naming survived to week 15
Why that structure, and what it cost Outcome 15.2 — and you will have under-rehearsed the cost
What breaks if a medium is removed Outcome 15.1, verified live rather than claimed
A departure from your week 14 plan Whether outcome 14.2's honesty holds under questioning
What you would change with another week Whether you can still see your own work

The answer that fails

"It looked better."

That is week 1's failure, in the final week of the course. Fifteen weeks exist to replace that sentence with a reason, and the presentation is largely where we find out whether it happened.

Use the prep sheet. It is built for this — it asks you to write down decisions and reasons rather than a running order.

6 · Two grades that never mix

Scores Of the project
Project rubric Components A–H 85%
Presentation rubric Component I 15%

They are scored separately and never averaged.

Making the work and accounting for the work are different competencies. Averaging would let a strong presentation carry weak media — and let a strong maker who cannot explain their decisions pass an outcome they have not met.

A good presentation cannot rescue a page that does not work. A working page does not excuse a defence you cannot give.

7 · Verify publication first

Outcome 15.1 requires the page published. Live, reachable, not local.

"It works on my machine" is not published.

Check it at the start of the week, from a device that is not yours and a network that is not the one you built it on. A page that will not load is the most avoidable way to lose 8% of a course grade, and there is no week 16.

Module 5 vocabulary

No new terms. These are the ones this week turns on, all in the course glossary:

Term Means
Landing page The published page presenting your whole body of work. It is the integrated piece. Component E
Positioning statement Who the work is for and what conventions it follows. 300–500 words. Component F
Removal test Delete one medium. If nothing breaks, the media were assembled rather than integrated
Integrated · Assembled Interdependent · merely co-present
Linear · Non-linear One fixed order · the audience chooses the path
Critique Structured spoken assessment across technical, aesthetic, and narrative dimensions. Component H
Presentation The live 5–7 minute defence plus questions. Component I, on its own rubric
Crew log · Disclosure log Signed record of crewing · per-submission record of sources and AI assistance

The bars that matter this week. Landing page is barred from website, site, portfolio, and homepage. Positioning statement is barred from artist statement, reflection, rationale, and about page — see section 4.

Practise

Run positioning or reflection? before the phase. Sort statements into the two kinds, find the conventions a statement claims but the work does not follow, and tell a structural cost from a gain.

Round 1 is the one to do properly. The positioning statement is the only assessment of LO 10 in this course, and the most common way to lose it is to write a good document of the wrong kind.

Check yourself before the phase

  1. What must be final and in hand before this week begins?
  2. State the removal test, and say what you do with a deletion that breaks nothing.
  3. What one question tells you whether your page is linear or non-linear?
  4. Name three things that belong in a reflection and not in a positioning statement.
  5. Why are the two rubrics never averaged?
  6. What does "it looked better" fail to be?

If you cannot answer 3, re-read section 3. Outcome 15.2 is a justification, and you cannot justify a structure you have not identified.

What happens in the session

Publication is verified first. The removal test is run on your page, in the room. Critique across all three dimensions, for the fourth and final time. Then presentations — 5 to 7 minutes plus questions, including at least two you did not prepare for.

Full detail in the lab sheet, the prep sheet, and checkpoint 4.

This is the last contact. Whatever is not evidenced here is not evidenced.
