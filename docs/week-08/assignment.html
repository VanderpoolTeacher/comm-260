Week 8 Assignment — Team production

COMM 260 · Introduction to Digital Media Production

Module summative · 15% of your course grade · midterm checkpoint

This is the largest single grade in the course outside the final project, and it is the only assessment of collaboration and project management anywhere in these fifteen weeks.

Week 7's formative work must be submitted before this. Accepted late without penalty until this summative is submitted; not accepted after.

What to submit

Submitted by
Planning documents — treatment, shot list, storyboard Team One set per team
Footage and separately recorded audio Team Backed up before anyone left the room
Role reflection Individual This is what carries your own evidence

The team production record is not part of this submission — your team handed it to your instructor at the end of the lab.

What a complete answer looks like

The role reflection is the part students get wrong, so here is one that would be marked Meets, in full:

Rotation 1 — I was on sound. I was responsible for the recording, which meant placing the microphone and watching levels. I moved the recorder twice because the first position picked up the corridor door. During the second setup I went and got room tone while Priya and Sam were rigging the next shot, because we were going to need it and nobody was using me.

Rotation 2 — I produced. I owned the day for this stretch. The studio was booked over by another group for forty minutes, so I re-sequenced: we shot the two corridor shots first, which did not need the room, and moved the desk shots to after. I told both of them what I was doing and why rather than just redirecting them. We came out of the rotation roughly where the schedule said we would be.

With twenty minutes left I dropped the close-up on the faders. We had decided in advance that it was the one we would lose, so it was a decision rather than a scramble, and I said out loud which shot was going and why.

Rotation 3 — I was on camera. The image was mine. I called for a second take on the wide because focus drifted halfway through the first one, rather than waiting to be asked.

What that demonstrates: each rotation names a responsibility and then what was actually done with it. The producer rotation shows sequencing decisions — what got reordered, what ran in parallel, what was dropped and why. And the camera rotation shows accountability without waiting to be asked.

Notice what is not in it: no account of whether the shoot went well, and no description of the piece. Those are not what this is evidencing.

To reach Exceeds, the producer rotation is where it happens — this one is close. It names a re-sequence and a deliberate drop. What would push it over is naming what the re-sequence cost: "shooting the corridor first meant we lost the light we had wanted for it, and I took that over losing two desk shots."

How this is graded

Assessed on your planning documents, and your team's account of who ran what — including the day you were producer.

Your teammates' account is what makes an individual grade possible from a shared production. A student whose teammates cannot say what they were responsible for has not evidenced outcome 8.5, whatever their own reflection claims.

That cuts both ways, and it is worth knowing in advance: doing a lot of work quietly does not evidence this outcome. Being the person who silently fixed everything, on a shoot where nobody could afterwards say what you were responsible for, is Approaching — not because the work did not happen, but because accountability that nobody can name is not accountability.

Why this one matters more than its weight

This is the only assessment of LO 9 in the course.

Outcome 8.3 — scheduling and running a shoot other people depend on — is the only place the project management half of that outcome is taught or assessed anywhere in these fifteen weeks.

The crew log in the final project shows you working on someone else's production. It never makes you accountable for a shared result. This week does, once.

There is no second chance at it later in the term.

What does not count

  • A shot list that does not come from the treatment. For every line, there should be a sentence that asked for it.
  • A plan nobody consulted. Departing from it is legitimate; not noticing that you departed is not.
  • A reflection with no first person in it. "The team decided" evidences nothing about you.
  • A reflection that is a summary of the shoot. The question is what you were responsible for.
  • Holding the producer role and standing still. Producing is deciding what happens next.
  • Audio recorded into the camera. It removes one of week 9's outcomes, for all three of you.
  • A smooth shoot offered as the answer. Nothing going wrong is not evidence of managing anything.

Use the glossary

This week: Team production, Planning documents, Deliverable, Module summative, Checkpoint.

The glossary bars pre-production, prep, plan alone, and paperwork as substitutes for planning documents — and it bars group project, group work, and collaboration exercise for team production.

On paperwork: the word is used in this week deliberately, and only ever as the name of the failure. Planning documents that get made to satisfy a requirement and are never opened again have become paperwork. That is not what they are called; it is what they turned into.

Rubric

LO 9 — Collaboration

Collaborate effectively in production teams, demonstrating communication and project management skills.

Level
Exceeds The producer day shows sequencing decisions — what ran in parallel, what changed when something failed
Meets Held a defined role and the producer role. Your team can say what you were responsible for
Approaching Present and contributing, but accountability is unclear or was never held
Not yet Teammates cannot say what you were responsible for

LO 4 — Video

Plan, shoot, and edit high-definition video using professional production techniques.

Level
Exceeds Departures are clearly decisions — you can say what the location turned out to be, and what you chose instead
Meets Plans, shoots, and edits. The piece is traceable to the plan and departures are named with reasons
Approaching Plan and piece do not obviously relate, or no departures are reported
Not yet No plan, or a plan written after the shoot — detectable because it contains no departures

The same four levels apply to every assignment in this course. Full descriptors and the whole map of which assignment evidences what: competency rubric.
