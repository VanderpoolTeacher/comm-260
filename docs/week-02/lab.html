Week 2 Lab — Build a reference collection

COMM 260 · Introduction to Digital Media Production

Student-facing. Bring one published photograph you think is good. Do not bring a camera.

What you are doing

Hunting down published images — other people's work, not your own — that demonstrate each term on the list. Then defending why each one qualifies.

You are not photographing anything this week. You cannot compose deliberately until you can see composition in work that already exists. Students who skip this step spend weeks 3 and 4 making choices by instinct and then cannot explain them — and explaining them is what the final project grades.

The eleven terms

subject emphasis subject placement
balance negative space
leading the eye framing within the frame
cropping shallow depth of field
deep depth of field frozen motion
motion blur

Part 1 · The images you brought

We open on the photographs you brought in. You will be asked why yours is good.

Then we take the answers apart and rewrite them as named decisions — the same exercise as week 1, now with a fixed vocabulary to use.

Part 2 · The hunt

Published work only. Not your own photographs. Not your camera roll.

For each term, find one image and write:

  1. The term, named
  2. The source, credited — maker and where you found it
  3. One sentence on what that choice does for that picture

For subject emphasis only, your sentence must also say which of placement, scale, focus, contrast, or light is doing the most work. That term has an extra demand and it is the one the week is built around.

Part 3 · Argue the hard ones

Bring the images you were unsure about to the room. We put them up and argue them out.

Does this actually demonstrate negative space, or is it just empty?

This is the part that counts. An image you had to argue for is worth more than one you found in four seconds.

Before you leave

Credit every source properly. This is the first week attribution is required, and the habit starts here — the final project's disclosure log is the same habit at the end of the term.

What you hand in

See the week 2 assignment.

One image per term, eleven in total. You are not photographing anything.

Two words that are not the same thing

Subject placement Subject emphasis
Where the subject sits in the frame What makes it read first
One decision Five means — placement, scale, focus, contrast, light
A centred subject is placed centrally A centred subject may still not dominate

Placement is one of the five means of emphasis. It is not a synonym for it, and treating them as the same thing is the single most common failure of this week.
