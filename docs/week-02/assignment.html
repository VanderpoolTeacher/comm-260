Week 2 Assignment — Found-image collection

COMM 260 · Introduction to Digital Media Production

Formative · submitted and critiqued · 0% of your course grade

This is required. It carries no grade, but it must be submitted before week 4's module summative. It is accepted late without penalty until that summative is submitted, and not accepted after.

What to submit

A collection of found images — one per term, eleven in total.

For each image:

  1. The term, named
  2. The source, credited — maker and where you found it
  3. One sentence on what that choice does for that picture

For subject emphasis, the sentence must also name which of placement, scale, focus, contrast, or light is doing the most work.

Published work only. Not your own photographs.

What a complete entry looks like

Negative space. — Fan Ho, Approaching Shadow, 1954. Sourced from the M+ collection.

The whole upper half of the frame is an empty diagonal of shadow, and the figure sits small against the bottom edge. The emptiness is not background — it is the weight pressing on her, and shrinking it would take the meaning out of the picture.

Note what that sentence does. It says what the choice does for that picture — not what negative space is in general.

How this is graded

Assessed on whether the image genuinely demonstrates the term, and whether you can say what it does.

A textbook example found quickly scores lower than a real one you had to argue for.

You are not photographing anything this week, and nothing from the surveyed list is graded. Manual mode, white balance, ISO, RAW versus JPEG, and file management were named in class so the words exist. They are not on this assignment. They return in week 7.

What does not count

  • First-result searching. Typing the term into an image search and submitting the top result. It usually qualifies and it teaches you nothing.
  • Your own photographs. This week is about seeing other people's decisions.
  • A definition instead of an observation. "Negative space is empty area around the subject" describes the term. Say what it does in this image.
  • Subject emphasis answered as placement. Placement is one of five means. Say which one dominates.
  • Uncredited sources. Attribution is part of the deliverable.

Use the glossary

Check your words against the glossary before you submit. Pay attention to the Never use column.

Two entries matter most this week: Subject emphasis and Subject placement. They are listed separately because conflating them is the mistake this assignment is built to catch.

Rubric

LO 1 — Aesthetic and narrative principles

Apply aesthetic and narrative principles to create compelling digital media projects.

Level
Exceeds Names the choice, what it accomplishes, and what the alternative would have cost
Meets Names the choice in course vocabulary and says what it accomplishes
Approaching Describes the work accurately but does not name the choice — or names the technique without saying what it does
Not yet States a preference. "It looked better."

LO 8 — Critical evaluation

Critically evaluate media projects for technical quality, aesthetic design, and narrative effectiveness.

Level
Exceeds Distinguishes preference from what the work does not achieve, and names what was lost
Meets All three dimensions, one specific and actionable point each
Approaching Warm, general and true — or three dimensions all making the same observation
Not yet Praise. Not actionable, satisfies no dimension

The same four levels apply to every assignment in this course. Full descriptors and the whole map of which assignment evidences what: competency rubric.
