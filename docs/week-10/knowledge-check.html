Week 10 Knowledge Check — What it covers

COMM 260 · Introduction to Digital Media Production

Module summative · scored, as part of week 10's 5% — together with the written comparison.

This is the only written test in this course. You have no prior experience of what one here looks like, so this sheet tells you exactly what is on it.

Read this well before the session. It is your review sheet.

What is covered

This list is complete. Nothing outside it is on the paper.

Area From What you should be able to do
Shot sizes and what each does Week 7 Name them, and say what each one does for a viewer
Frame rate and shutter Week 7 Say what each controls, and why they are not the same number
Sequence settings Week 9 Say why the sequence has to match the footage, and what you see when it does not
Sync Week 9 Say why audio is recorded separately, and what the alignment problem is
Export specifications Week 9 Say what each setting controls — resolution, frame rate, codec, bitrate, container
Linear and non-linear structure Week 10 Define both, and name what each costs

Study those six things. Not everything from weeks 7 to 10 — these six.

What kind of questions

Not recall. You will not be asked to list three shot sizes.

Understanding. You will be given a situation and asked what is happening and what you would change.

You will not be asked You will be asked
Name three shot sizes. A scene opens on a close-up of someone's hands and never shows the room. What does the viewer not know, and when would that be the right choice?
What is bitrate? Your export meets the stated resolution and frame rate but the file is enormous. Which setting would you change, and what would it cost you?

Six to eight questions.

At least one question requires you to name a cost

This is the same criterion the written comparison is marked on, and it is deliberate — the test and the lab assess the same thing two different ways.

On any question that asks what a choice costs:

Naming only the upside does not count.

A cost is something you would rather not have paid. If what you write sounds like a feature, it is not a cost yet.

Not a cost A cost
"You lose control of the order, which empowers the audience." "I cannot guarantee anyone sees the first image before the second, and the comparison is the whole point."
"A higher bitrate means better quality." "A higher bitrate roughly doubles my file size, and this is going on a landing page that has to load."

How it is marked

Questions requiring a cost are marked in two steps:

  1. Was a cost named at all?
  2. Is it the right one?

Most marks are lost at step one.

How to prepare

  • Work the six areas above, and nothing else
  • Run gain or cost? — round 3 is the cost question, rehearsed
  • Re-read your own week 9 tools statement. If you wrote real reasons there, you have already done most of the export and sequence revision
  • Write your own one-sentence definitions of linear and non-linear before the session — outcome 10.1 is in your own words, and a definition you drafted yourself is one you can still produce under pressure

Why this is only 5%

It sits between two 15% practicals — weeks 8 and 11 — and well below both, on purpose.

Those weeks assess whether you can do the work. This assesses whether you know what you were doing and why, which a finished file never shows on its own. It should not outweigh the work whose understanding it is checking.
