Week 10 Assignment — Written comparison

COMM 260 · Introduction to Digital Media Production

Module summative · 5% of your course grade

This assessment has two parts and they are scored separately: this written comparison, and the knowledge check you sat in the session.

Week 9's formative work must be submitted before this. Accepted late without penalty until this summative is submitted; not accepted after.

What to submit

One written comparison of your two structures. Four things, in any order you like:

  1. What changed between the linear and non-linear versions
  2. What each structure gained
  3. What each structure cost
  4. Which one you would publish, and why — argued against the one you rejected

What a complete answer looks like

A comparison that would be marked Meets, in full. The material was ten photographs documenting a bicycle repair shop over one day.

What changed. The linear version runs the day in order — the shutters going up, the tools laid out, the first customer, the work, the shop at dusk. The non-linear version has three entry points: the shop itself, the person, and the work. From any of them you can reach the other two.

What linear gained. A build. Putting the exterior at dusk last only means anything if you have already seen the same doorway in the morning, so I could hold that comparison back and spend it at the end. I also knew nobody would meet the person before they had seen the room, which made the room feel like it belonged to someone.

What linear cost. Everything depends on getting through it. If someone stops after three images they have seen a shop and no person at all, and the piece is about the person.

What non-linear gained. Somebody who cares about the repairs can go straight to the repairs. Nobody has to sit through the parts they did not come for, and three different people get three different pieces out of the same ten images.

What non-linear cost. The dusk photograph stops working. It is the same doorway as the morning one and that is the whole reason it is in the set — but I cannot guarantee anyone sees the morning one first, so for a lot of visitors it is just a photograph of a door at night. I lost the comparison, which was the strongest thing I had.

What I would publish, and why. Linear. Not because it is simpler — because this set has exactly one idea in it and the idea is a comparison between two moments. The non-linear version is genuinely better for the person who came looking for the repairs, and I would rather serve the idea than that person. If the set had been a catalogue of the work rather than a day in the shop, I would have chosen the other one.

What that demonstrates: every cost is specific to this material, not to structure in general. The non-linear cost names exactly what broke and why it mattered. And the final paragraph argues against the rejected version on its own terms — it concedes what non-linear was better at before choosing linear anyway.

To reach Exceeds, defend the choice harder against the alternative. This answer is close. What would push it over is engaging with the strongest version of the rejected option: "the best case for non-linear here is that most visitors will not finish a linear piece anyway, so a build nobody reaches is worth less than three routes people actually take — I think that is wrong for a ten-image set, but it would be right at forty."

How this is graded

Assessed on the knowledge check, plus whether the written comparison names what each structure costs, not only what it gains.

Naming only the upside does not count. That is stated in the course outline and it is the thing this assessment exists to check.

Two things are being judged and they are different. The knowledge check covers the module's concepts across weeks 7–10. The comparison covers outcome 10.3 specifically.

What does not count

  • A cost that is a gain restated. "You lose control, which empowers the audience." If it sounds like a feature, it is not a cost.
  • Gains only. Both halves of outcome 10.3, both structures.
  • A cost that is generic. "Non-linear is harder to build" is true of everything and tells me nothing about your material.
  • A defence that is praise. Outcome 10.4 asks you to argue against the version you rejected, not to compliment the one you kept.
  • A comparison of two things that are not the same material. Same items, both structures. That was the constraint.
  • Non-linear described as interactive, branching, random access, or modular. All four are barred.

Use the glossary

This week: Linear, Non-linear, Cost, Deliberate choice, Module summative, Knowledge check.

Check the Never use column before you write. It bars interactive, branching, random access, and modular for non-linear, and sequential, chronological, and traditional for linear. Students reach for every one of them, and each swap quietly replaces the concept with something adjacent.

Differentiate between linear and non-linear storytelling methods and apply them appropriately in media projects.
