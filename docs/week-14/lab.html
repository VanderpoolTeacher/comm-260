COMM 260 · Introduction to Digital Media Production

What you are doing

Weeks 7, 8 and 9 compressed into one phase and run alone. Plan, shoot, edit, export — 60 to 90 seconds, to your own style guide.

The difference from week 8 is that nobody is waiting on you. In week 8, two people were, for two-thirds of the shoot — and that pressure is also what kept the day moving. On your own the day quietly expands until the light goes, and nobody will tell you it is happening.

Schedule your shoot day as if somebody were waiting.

Part 1 · Plan the break — in the room, on the board, together

This phase loses two sessions to Thanksgiving. Work this out now rather than discovering it.

On the board, for each of you:

Two rules that come out of this every year:

  1. Book equipment for the whole phase, including the days nobody is on campus. The desk closes and the calendar does not stretch afterwards.
  2. Do not plan to shoot during the break unless your subject has already confirmed. People are away, buildings are locked, and a shoot day nobody attends is not recoverable inside this phase.

Whatever you can finish before the break, finish before the break. Your checkpoint feedback arrives compressed against week 15 regardless.

Part 2 · Know which of the three you are about to do

Produces
Follow the plan rigidly A piece that ignores what the location actually offered
Abandon it silently A piece with no traceable reasoning
Depart deliberately, and say so Outcome 14.2

Rigid is not the safe option. Your treatment says the workshop is busy; you arrive and it is empty, and it is a better piece about solitude. Shooting your shot list anyway because it is your shot list ignores the only real information the day gave you.

Keep a note as you go. Not afterwards — on the day, when you notice. What you planned, what you did instead, and why.

There is no penalty in this course for a plan that changed. There is one for a plan that changed and was not noticed.

Part 3 · Shoot it

Everything here you did in weeks 7 and 8:

  • Camera configured with reasons — frame rate, shutter, resolution
  • Coverage — more than one size on the moments that matter
  • Audio recorded separately, not into the camera
  • Tripod where a locked shot is the decision

Part 4 · Read your own pacing rules before you cut

Maximum shot length. Cut speed. Whether music sits under speech. How long a still is held.

This is the part of your style guide you have never had to obey — there has been no video to obey it with. Students obey their visual identity faithfully and discover at the checkpoint that their video complies with none of the rules they wrote for it.

Go and read your own document.

And if a pacing rule turns out to be unworkable, week 13's rule still applies: revise it, and record it. Silently ignoring it is the option that is not available.

Part 5 · Cut and export

Week 9, alone:

  • Sync the separate audio, verified on headphones
  • Cut on action where the action allows
  • Export to the stated specification — still posted from week 9
  • Verify it plays on a machine that is not yours

Part 6 · The project file has to open

Component D is submitted with its project file, and it gets opened at the checkpoint, in front of you.

Week 9's outcome 9.2 is what makes that possible:

A project file that will not open evidences nothing. Not "loses marks." Nothing — because outcome 14.1 is plan, shoot and edit, and the edit is only inspectable from the project.

Caught at the checkpoint it is fixable. Caught in week 15 it is not, and it costs you the heaviest component in the project.

Part 7 · Crewing — the last chance

Third session. Third different producer. Signed on the day.

There is no phase four for crewing. If you have duplicated a producer across phases 1 and 2, fix it this week — it cannot be fixed later.
