Week 11 Lab — Build one piece that survives the removal test

COMM 260 · Introduction to Digital Media Production

This happens in the room. Bring three things you have already made — a photograph from week 3, your audio from week 6, your video from week 9 — on storage you control.

Confirm they open before you arrive. This is the week's entire raw material and there is no substitute for it.

If you are missing one of the three

Say so before the lab starts, not halfway through it.

There is a set of media held for exactly this case — a photograph, an audio piece, and a short video, deliberately unrelated to each other. Building from those demonstrates outcomes 11.1 and 11.3 fully, and 11.2 partially, since the media are not yours.

That costs you far less than losing the session. This is a 15% assessment and it is the only one of its kind in the course.

What you are doing

Taking your three pieces and building one piece from them — one where all three are load-bearing.

Then breaking it on purpose. Three times.

The form is open. It may be a timeline, a page, a sequence. What matters is not the container; it is whether removing any one of the three takes something with it.

Part 1 · Fill in Part 1 of the sheet — before you build anything

Open the removal test sheet.

Write down what job each medium is doing that the other two cannot. One line each, specific to your material.

"Sets the mood" is not a job. It is a hope.

"The photograph is the only place you can look at his hands for longer than a second" is a job.

Then predict what would break if you deleted each one.

Do this first. In about an hour you are going to test these predictions, and if you write them afterwards there is nothing to test.

Part 2 · Build it

All three media present, all three doing the job you wrote down.

Watch for the two things that will happen by default:

The default failure What it looks like
Two media saying the same thing Your audio describes what your video shows. It will feel tight and deliberate while you are doing it
The decorative third A still sitting behind the video, dimmed. Present because the brief said three

Both feel like good work from the inside. That is why the test exists.

Part 3 · Delete, three times

Actually delete. Actually play it back. Not in your head.

  1. Remove the photograph. Play it. What broke?
  2. Remove the audio. Play it. What broke?
  3. Remove the video. Play it. What broke?

Record each answer on the sheet.

A deletion that breaks nothing is the finding, not the failure.

You will want to hide it. Do not. A correctly diagnosed failure scores better than an untested claim — that is in the rubric, in those words.

Part 4 · Where your prediction was wrong

Compare what you wrote in Part 1 against what actually happened.

The deletion that surprised you is the most useful thing in this lab. A medium you thought was carrying something and was not is a diagnosis already half written.

Part 5 · Swap with a partner

They delete. You watch.

You are lenient with your own work — everyone is. You know what your piece was supposed to do, and you hear that intention even when it is not in the piece. Your partner only knows what it does.

Take their answers seriously, especially the ones less generous than yours.

Part 6 · Revise, if there is time

The fix is almost always subtraction.

If your audio is removable because the video already says it, adding more audio gives you more of the same doubling. Take the duplication out of the video — and the audio suddenly has to carry something.

Removing is faster than adding, and it teaches you what the principle actually is.

Before you leave

What you hand in

See the assignment.

The integrated piece, and a written removal-test analysis — which medium carries what, and what breaks if you delete each one.

Three deletions, three findings. The analysis is not a summary of your piece.
