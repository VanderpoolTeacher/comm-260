Week 11 Assignment — Integrated piece and removal-test analysis

COMM 260 · Introduction to Digital Media Production

Module summative · 15% of your course grade

This is the only assessment of integration in the course, and it is the last week before the final project.

Week 9's formative work must be submitted before this. Accepted late without penalty until this summative is submitted; not accepted after.

What to submit

  1. The integrated piece — built from your photograph, your audio, and your video
  2. The written removal-test analysis — which medium carries what, and what breaks if you delete each one

Three deletions, three findings. The analysis is not a summary of your piece and it is not optional.

What a complete answer looks like

An analysis that would be marked Meets, in full. This student's piece did not survive.

What each medium was carrying.

The photograph is my week 3 image of the empty bench outside the sports hall. Its job is to hold one thing still — you can look at the worn patch on the seat for as long as you want, and nothing moves you on.

The audio is forty seconds from my week 6 piece, where my grandmother talks about waiting for people. Its job is interiority — she says what she was thinking, which nothing I could photograph would show.

The video is my week 9 piece, cut down to twenty seconds: people crossing the concourse, nobody stopping.

Deletion 1 — remove the photograph. Something breaks. The audio is about waiting, and without a still image there is nowhere to wait. The video keeps moving, so the whole piece becomes about motion and the word "waiting" has nothing to attach to.

Deletion 2 — remove the audio. Something breaks, badly. A bench and some people walking. There is no reason for either of them to be there.

Deletion 3 — remove the video. Nothing changed. I played it three times to be sure.

Where my prediction was wrong. I wrote in Part 1 that the video was carrying "the contrast between people moving and the bench being still." It is not. The photograph already carries the stillness on its own — it is a still image, that is what it is — so the video was providing a contrast that did not need providing. My partner deleted it first and said she could not tell what it had been for, which was less kind than my own answer and more accurate.

Diagnosis: two media saying the same thing. The photograph and the video were both making the same point about stillness and motion, from opposite sides. The photograph makes it structurally, just by being a photograph. The video makes it literally, by showing movement. The literal one is redundant, and it is the one that goes.

What I would change. Not the video — the photograph. If the photograph were of something moving that I had frozen, then the stillness would be my decision rather than a property of the format, and the video would have something to be in tension with. That is a reshoot, not an edit, so for this piece I would cut the video and submit two media that both work.

What that demonstrates: every deletion was performed, not predicted. The failure is stated plainly and then diagnosed — it names which two media duplicated each other and why one was redundant. And the fix correctly identifies that the problem is not the medium that was removable.

To reach Exceeds, say specifically what would break in the fixed version. This answer is close. What would push it over: "if the photograph were a frozen motion, deleting the video would then break the piece — because the video is what tells you the concourse is normally in motion, and without it a frozen photograph is just a sharp photograph."

How this is graded

Assessed on whether the piece survives the removal test, and whether the analysis identifies which medium carries what.

Two things, and you can pass one and not the other.

A piece that survives, with an analysis that merely describes it Has not demonstrated outcome 11.3
A piece that does not survive, with an analysis that correctly diagnoses why Has demonstrated the harder outcome, and is graded accordingly rather than failed

A correctly diagnosed failure outranks an untested claim.

That is in the competency rubric, in those words. Do not protect your piece. Test it.

What does not count

  • "It survives" with no deletions performed. The test is a thing you do, not a thing you conclude.
  • An analysis that restates what the piece is about. The question is what each medium carries and what breaks without it.
  • A third medium that is decorative. A still behind the video, a title card. Present is not the same as load-bearing.
  • Two media saying the same thing. Narration describing the image is the default failure and it is not a small one.
  • Fixing by addition. If a medium is removable, more of it is not the answer.
  • Reporting incoherence as an integration failure. A piece can pass the removal test and still be bad. Those are separate findings and only one of them is this week's.

Use the glossary

This week: Removal test, Integrated, Assembled.

Check the Never use column. It bars combined, mixed, multimedia, and blended for integrated, and layered, stacked, and thrown together for assembled. Every one of those words lets you avoid saying which state your piece is actually in — which is the only question being asked.

This is week 15's dry run

Outcome 15.1 is integrate three media into a landing page that passes the removal test. The same sentence, at full scale, with your whole final project attached.

This week exists so that if your integration does not work, you find out now — at 15%, with three pieces you already own — rather than in week 15 while everything is being judged at once.

Rubric

LO 7 — Integration

Integrate multiple media forms (photography, video, audio) into cohesive digital productions.

Level
Exceeds The division of labour is deliberate, and the analysis says specifically what would break
Meets Each medium carries something the others cannot. Removing any one breaks the piece
Approaching All media present, but removing one changes nothing — assembled, not integrated
Not yet Two media saying the same thing, or a third that is decorative

A correctly diagnosed failure outranks an untested claim. A student whose piece does not survive but who identifies precisely why has demonstrated the harder half of this competency, and is graded accordingly.

The same four levels apply to every assignment in this course. Full descriptors and the whole map of which assignment evidences what: competency rubric.
