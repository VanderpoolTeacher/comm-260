Week 6 Assignment — 60-second edited piece

COMM 260 · Introduction to Digital Media Production

Module summative · 10% of your course grade

What to submit

  1. Your edited piece — 60 seconds
  2. Your raw sources — the week 5 recordings you built it from

Both. Always. An edit submitted without its sources cannot be assessed, because the claim being made is that the edit is better than the raw — and that is checkable only against the raw.

This is the same pairing the final project's audio piece requires in week 13. That is not a coincidence: this week is that requirement's dry run, at 10% instead of the final project's weight.

How this is graded

Assessed on whether the edit is demonstrably better than the raw.

You submit both, so it is checkable rather than asserted.

What does not count

  • Processed speech. If every breath is gone it sounds uncanny before it sounds clear.
  • Noise reduction as the main edit. Pushed hard it swirls. The real fix was at the microphone.
  • Music that loses you a word.
  • A reorder with no reason. Outcome 6.2 requires the structural cut and saying why.
  • An edit without raw sources. Not assessable.
  • Unused room tone, if your joins need it.

Use the glossary

This week: Raw sources — unedited originals, kept and submitted. The glossary bars originals, masters, source files, and unedited as substitutes.

Rubric

LO 5 — Audio

Record and edit audio with attention to clarity, quality, and storytelling impact.

Level
Exceeds A structural decision changes how the piece lands, and you can say what it cost
Meets Audibly better than the raw — clear, and shaped by a structural decision. Raw sources submitted
Approaching Cleanly cut, but says what the raw said. Nothing structural happened
Not yet Processed speech, noise reduction as the main edit, or no raw sources

LO 1 — Aesthetic and narrative principles

Apply aesthetic and narrative principles to create compelling digital media projects.

Level
Exceeds Names the choice, what it accomplishes, and what the alternative would have cost
Meets Names the choice in course vocabulary and says what it accomplishes
Approaching Describes the work accurately but does not name the choice — or names the technique without saying what it does
Not yet States a preference. "It looked better."

The same four levels apply to every assignment in this course. Full descriptors and the whole map of which assignment evidences what: competency rubric.
