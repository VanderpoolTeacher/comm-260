Week 6 Lab — Build a 60-second piece

COMM 260 · Introduction to Digital Media Production

Student-facing. Bring your week 5 recordings — all three, plus your room tone. Bring closed-back headphones.

What you are doing

Building a 60-second piece from the recordings you made last week, and proving it is better than what you started with.

You submit the edit and the raw sources. That is what makes "better" checkable rather than something you assert.

Part 1 · Choose your source

You have three recordings of the same script. Pick the one you will build from — and be ready to say why.

Part 2 · Cut for clarity

Remove false starts, long breaths, stumbles.

Do not remove all of them. Speech cut too tight stops sounding like a person thinking. If it sounds processed, you have gone too far — and the usual cause is that the breaths are gone.

Part 3 · Cut for meaning

Now make a structural cut. Move the strongest line to the front. Cut an explanation and let the listener work it out. End before the resolution.

Be ready to say what the new order does that the old one did not — and what it costs.

Part 4 · Room tone

Lay your room tone under your joins.

Last week you were asked what it was for. Cut two takes together without it and listen to what happens at the join. Then put it under.

Part 5 · Music

Add a bed from the provided library.

Music loud enough to be felt, quiet enough that no word is lost. There is no number for this. Set it by listening, on headphones, and then check it on speakers.

Part 6 · Noise reduction, last and least

If you need it, use the smallest amount that works.

Pushed hard, noise reduction makes speech swirl. It is the most common failure in this assignment.

The real fix was last week, at the microphone.

Before you leave

Play your edit against your raw source, back to back.

If you cannot hear the improvement, your grader will not either — and that comparison is what this week is assessed on.

What you hand in

See the week 6 assignment.

Your edited piece plus your raw sources. Both. The same pairing the final project requires in week 13.
