Part 1 — Brand Guide

Positioning

I make ____________________ for ____________________.

Fill both blanks. If you can't, you don't have a brand yet — write five versions and keep the one you'd defend out loud.

The professional context I'm aiming at

Be specific. "Wedding videography for couples in northwest Ohio," not "video." "Short-form food content for independent restaurants," not "social media."

Audience

Who sees this work and decides whether to hire you? One or two sentences. Not "everyone."

Voice

Three adjectives are not a voice. Instead: write one sentence the way you would say it to a client, and one sentence you would never say.

I sound like this: "..."

I would never say: "..."

What I make

The two or three things you actually produce. Name them concretely.

Photography rules

Rule My value
Aperture e.g. f/2.8 or wider
Lighting e.g. natural light only, no on-camera flash
Framing e.g. subject off-centre, never dead centre
Orientation e.g. landscape for wide scenes, portrait for people
Colour treatment e.g. warm grade, greens desaturated

Audio rules

Rule My value
Music tempo e.g. under 90 BPM
Music mood e.g. instrumental only, no vocals under speech
Speech level e.g. peaks −12 to −6 dBFS
Room tone e.g. 10 seconds captured at every location

Video rules

Rule My value
Frame rate e.g. 24 fps
Cutting e.g. cut on action, never mid-sentence
Camera movement e.g. locked off or slow push only, no handheld
Shot length e.g. nothing under 2 seconds
Titles e.g. lower thirds only, no full-screen cards

The test

Give this page to someone who has never seen your work. Show them a photograph.

Can they tell you whether it's on-brand or off-brand?

  • Yes — you've written a specification. Good.
  • No — you've written adjectives. Go back and put numbers and names in.

You will shoot against these rules for three weeks and be graded against them. Vague rules cannot be followed and cannot be checked.
