Checkpoint 1 — Brand foundation

COMM 260 · Introduction to Digital Media Production

Formative checkpoint · project rubric, component A

10% of the project · 4% of the course

What to submit

  1. Logo
  2. Brand guide — positioning, audience, voice, what you make
  3. Style guide — checkable values across all five areas
  4. Landing page shell, reachable at the publishing target
  5. Project folder, set up to the naming convention

Plus, in the room: critique across all three dimensions, as taught in week 4. That is component H, and it runs at every checkpoint.

How this is graded

Assessed chiefly on whether the style guide holds values a photograph could actually be checked against.

Everything else in this component is quicker to judge and worth less attention than that one criterion — because the style guide is the document weeks 13, 14 and 15 are measured against, and an unusable guide produces three weeks of unassessable media.

What a complete answer looks like

A style guide that would be marked Meets, in full. It is not long — completeness here is coverage, not volume.

Positioning. I make short documentary portraits for regional arts organisations.

Colour. #2B2724 text · #F4EFE8 ground · #C4703A accent, used once per page and never behind type.

Type. Söhne 600 for headings at 32px and above. Söhne 400 at 17px for body, 1.55 line height. No third face.

Photography. Available light only — no flash. Nothing above 5600K. Shallow depth for portraits, f/2.8 or wider. Subject placed on a third; horizon never centred. Saturation reduced roughly 20% in post, and no colour left of the neutral axis in the shadows.

Video. No shot longer than 6 seconds. Cut on action where the action allows. Stills held 4 seconds minimum, never longer than 8.

Audio. Speech peaks between −12 and −6 dBFS, recorded within 30cm of the source. Music never sits under speech — beds only between spoken sections.

What that demonstrates: every line could be checked by a stranger. Five areas covered, including the two students routinely omit. And "saturation reduced roughly 20%" shows the honest case — some values are a range, and a range is still enough for someone to say that is clearly not muted.

Notice what is not in it: no adjectives doing load-bearing work. Warm, clean, intimate, considered, filmic — none of them appear, because none of them constrain anything.

How the checkpoint runs

1 Critique in the room, all three dimensions — component H
2 Your style guide is tested against a real photograph. If nobody can say whether it complies, that is the finding — and it is the point
3 Your landing page shell is confirmed reachable at the publishing target
4 Your project folder is checked against the naming convention
5 Your phase 1 crewing is confirmed booked — a producer's name, not an intention

What does not count

  • A style guide made of adjectives. Warm, natural, considered — if two people could disagree about whether an image complies, it is not a value.
  • A guide that only covers colour and type. Your audio and video are checked against this document too. Five areas.
  • A landing page shell with none of your identity on it. Outcome 12.3 is apply your identity to it. A default scaffold has not done that.
  • A shell that only exists locally. It has to be reachable. Week 15 requires it published, and a hosting problem found then costs you the component.
  • Positioning written backwards from work you already have. Reusing earlier work is fine. Positioning yourself around it is week 3's intent-after-shooting problem repeating.
  • Crewing understood but not booked. A name and a date, or it has not happened.

Use the glossary

This week: Phase, Checkpoint, Component, Container, Brand foundation, Brand guide, Style guide, Landing page, Crewing, Crew log.

Check the Never use column. Checkpoint is barred from being called a milestone, a deadline, a gate, or a review — and all four get used anyway. Style guide is barred from visual guide and look book, which is more than pedantry here: this guide is not visual only, and calling it a look book is how the audio and pacing rules go missing.

The container rule, said once more

The brand foundation evidences no learning outcome. It is required, it is graded, and it is worth 10% of the project.

That is deliberate. A beautiful container with thin media does not pass this course, and the weighting is what makes that arithmetic true rather than merely stated.

The logo is the most enjoyable thing in this phase and the most visibly finished. It is worth less than the video piece, which is 15% and two phases away.

Rubric

Component A is a container — it evidences no learning outcome, so no competency from the competency rubric is cited here.

It is scored on the project rubric, component A, against one question:

Does the style guide hold values that a photograph could be checked against?

The four levels are the same four used everywhere in this course:

Level
Exceeds All five areas hold checkable values, and the guide names what its own rules give up — a shallow-depth rule that admits it loses context
Meets All five areas hold values a stranger could check an image against
Approaching Colour and type are checkable; photography, video, or audio rules are adjectives — or missing
Not yet Adjectives throughout. Nobody could say whether any given image complies

The same four levels apply to every assignment in this course. Full descriptors: competency rubric.
