Week 12 · Phase 1 — Decide how you want to present yourself — Lesson

COMM 260 · Introduction to Digital Media Production

Read this before the session. About 25 minutes — plus the student brief, which is another half hour and not optional.

Four things before you arrive:

  1. Read the student brief in full — all four phases, and both rubrics. Not just this phase. You cannot book equipment or schedule crewing if you only know what happens next week.
  2. Draft your positioning sentence: "I make ___ for ___." Bring it. It will be taken apart, publicly, and that is the useful part.
  3. Find three producers whose work you would want yours mentioned alongside. Not to copy — to locate yourself. Bring links.
  4. Look at the landing page scaffold if you have never written HTML.

1 · Nothing here is new

Eleven weeks exist to make this one possible. This module teaches no technique for the first time.

Your photography set is week 3's task at twelve images. Your audio piece is weeks 5 and 6 at two to three minutes. Your video is weeks 7 through 9, alone. The landing page is week 11's removal test at full scale, and justifying its structure is week 10.

If you meet something for the first time in these four weeks, an earlier week did not do its job — say so, and we will deal with it as a gap rather than as your failure.

What is new is that you are now working to a specification. And you are the one who writes it.

2 · Positioning is not a job title

Outcome 12.1 asks you to state your positioning as "I make ___ for ___" and name your professional context specifically.

There are two ways this goes wrong, and almost everybody produces one of them on the first attempt.

Why it fails
"I make content for people." Too broad to constrain anything. It rules nothing out, so it cannot tell you what to shoot
"I'm a videographer." A job title, not a position. It says what you are called, not who the work is for
"I make short documentary portraits for regional arts organisations." A position. It tells you what to shoot, who for, and what conventions apply

The test is whether it rules something out. A positioning sentence that permits everything has decided nothing — which is exactly what week 8 said about a treatment, and for the same reason.

This is the same move you have made twice already: week 1 turned description into a named choice, week 4 turned preference into judgment. This turns a job title into a position.

3 · A style guide holds values that can be checked

This is the week's real work and the checkpoint's chief criterion.

Outcome 12.2 asks for a style guide with values that can be checked — hex codes, typefaces, aperture and pacing rules.

Here is the test, and it is the only one that matters:

Could a photograph be checked against this?

If you shoot twelve images and nobody could say whether they comply, the guide constrains nothing — and week 13 has nothing to be judged against.

Not checkable Checkable
"Warm and approachable" #C4703A primary, #F4EFE8 ground
"Clean, modern type" Söhne 600 for headings, Söhne 400 at 17px for body
"Intimate portraits" Shallow depth — f/2.8 or wider — for every portrait
"Natural light" Available light only. No flash. No colour temperature above 5600K
"Good pacing" No shot longer than 6 seconds. Music never under speech

Adjectives are not values. Muted, natural colour palette sounds specific and constrains nothing. Ask yourself for the hex codes.

The half everybody forgets

Your style guide must constrain audio and video too.

Outcome 12.2 names aperture and pacing rules explicitly, and students routinely write a beautiful visual identity guide that says nothing about sound or rhythm — then discover in week 14 that their video had no specification to obey.

Pacing rules to decide now: maximum shot length · how fast cuts run · whether music sits under speech · how long a still is held.

4 · Your guide will turn out to be wrong, and that is allowed

At some point in week 13 or 14 you will find that your own specification does not work — your guide says shallow depth and your creative intent needs the environment legible.

That is a real conflict and both resolutions are legitimate. Revise the guide, or revise the intent.

What is not legitimate is ignoring one and hoping nobody checks.

Revising your style guide is allowed. Revising it silently is not.

Record the revision and why. A student who discovers their own specification is unworkable has learned something worth more than compliance.

5 · The landing page shell

Outcome 12.3 is a shell with your identity applied — colours, type, structure. Not a finished page. The media do not exist yet.

The scaffold is there if you have never written HTML. It is a starting structure, not a finished design, and a scaffold with none of your colours or type on it has not met the outcome — the outcome is applying your identity to it.

Confirm it is reachable at the publishing target this week. Not published content — reachable. Week 15 requires the page published, and a hosting problem discovered in week 15 costs you the component.

6 · The project folder

Outcome 12.4. Dull, quick, and it is week 9's media management outcome applied to four weeks of work instead of one.

The reason is identical: a project that cannot be found cannot be submitted, and you are about to accumulate more media than you have handled all term.

7 · Two numbers, and the arithmetic that matters

Component weights are percentages of the project, not of the course.

Of the project Of the course
A · Brand foundation 10% 4%
D · Video piece 15% 6%
E · Landing page 20% 8%
The final project 100% 40%

And the container rule. The brand foundation — logo, brand guide, style guide — is a container: required, graded, and evidencing no learning outcome.

It is held at 10% on purpose. A beautiful container with thin media does not pass this course, and the weighting is what makes that true rather than merely stated.

The logo is the most enjoyable part of this week and the most visibly finished thing you will make. It is worth less than the video piece. Spend accordingly.

Module 5 vocabulary

Module 5 introduces no new craft vocabulary — it is where every earlier module's terms get used at once. These are the project's nouns:

Term Means
Phase One of the four week-long blocks inside the final project. A module is a block of the course; a phase is a block of the project
Checkpoint The end of a phase, where deliverables are submitted and critique happens. Four of them
Component One of the nine graded parts of the project, A–I
Container A component that is required and graded but evidences no learning outcome. The brand foundation is the only one
Brand foundation Logo, brand guide, and style guide together. Component A
Style guide The specific visual and sonic rules your media must follow — hex codes, typefaces, aperture and pacing rules
Landing page The published page presenting your whole body of work. Component E
Positioning statement Who the work is for and what conventions it follows. 300–500 words in week 15, component F — but the sentence it grows from is written this week
Crewing Working on a classmate's shoot in a support role
Crew log Running record of that work, signed by the producer you crewed for

All are in the course glossary. Checkpoint is barred from being called a milestone, a deadline, a gate, or a review — and all four get used anyway.

Practise

Run can you check it? before the session. Sort positioning sentences, convert adjectives into values, and find the half of a style guide that is missing. Unlimited attempts, no marks.

Round 2 is the one to do properly. It is adjective-into-value, which is the checkpoint's chief criterion.

Check yourself before the session

  1. What makes a positioning sentence a position rather than a job title?
  2. What is the test for whether a style guide value is checkable?
  3. Name three things a style guide must constrain that are not visual.
  4. Your guide and your creative intent contradict each other. What are your two legitimate options, and what is the illegitimate one?
  5. The landing page is 20%. Twenty per cent of what?
  6. Why is the brand foundation worth less than the video piece?

If you cannot answer 2, re-read section 3. It is what checkpoint 1 assesses.

What happens in the session

Positioning sentences go on the board and get rewritten live. Then you convert a vague style guide into a checkable one — and watch a real photograph held against a real guide to see whether anyone can tell if it complies.

Full detail in the lab sheet and checkpoint 1.

Schedule your phase 1 crewing before you leave. Three phases, three different producers. Students who hear this in week 14 cannot comply — there are not enough phases left.
