A checkpoint is formative in its timing, not its weight. What you submit carries into your final project mark. What is formative is that you get the feedback while there is still time to use it.

What you are doing

Writing the specification that weeks 13, 14 and 15 will be checked against.

This is the only week of the final project where you are not making media. Use it — a guide written carelessly today costs you three weeks of unassessable work.

Part 1 · Your positioning sentence, out loud

"I make ______________ for ______________."

Some sentences go on the board and get rewritten with the room. Volunteer yours if you can — it is faster to have it taken apart in five minutes than to discover in week 13 that it constrained nothing.

The test: does it rule something out?

Fails Why
"I make content for people." Too broad. It permits everything
"I'm a videographer." A job title, not a position
"I make short documentary portraits for regional arts organisations." Works. What to shoot, who for, what conventions

Part 2 · The brand guide

Open the brand guide sheet and fill in Part 1.

Positioning, audience, voice, and what you make. It is short, and if your positioning sentence is specific it mostly writes itself.

Voice is the one people struggle with. Three adjectives are not a voice. Write one sentence the way you would say it to a client, and one sentence you would never say.

Part 3 · The style guide — most of your time goes here

Part 2 of the same sheet.

The only test that matters:

Could a photograph be checked against this by someone who has never met you?

Convert every adjective into a value. If two people could look at the same image and disagree about whether it complies, it is not a value yet.

Not checkable Checkable
"Warm and approachable" #C4703A primary, #F4EFE8 ground
"Clean, modern type" Söhne 600 headings, 400 at 17px body
"Intimate portraits" Shallow depth — f/2.8 or wider — every portrait
"Good pacing" No shot over 6 seconds. Music never under speech

Cover all five areas. Most guides that fail this checkpoint fail by covering two:

The last two are the ones everybody forgets. Your video and audio pieces are checked against this document too.

Some values resist conversion, and that is honest. Get as close as you can — "saturation reduced roughly 20%" is enough for someone to say that is clearly not muted, which is all the guide has to do.

Part 4 · The landing page shell

Open the scaffold if you have never written HTML. Its README says what each file does.

A shell with your identity applied — your colours, your type, your structure. Not a finished page; you have no media yet.

A scaffold with none of your own colours or type on it has not met outcome 12.3. Applying the identity is the outcome.

Then confirm it is reachable at the publishing target. Not published content — reachable. Do this today.

Part 5 · Project folder and naming convention

Quick and dull. It is week 9's media management outcome applied to four weeks instead of one, and the reason is the same: a project that cannot be found cannot be submitted.

Set it up to the posted convention now, before there is anything in it.

Part 6 · Book your phase 1 crewing

Three sessions across weeks 12–14. Three different producers. One per phase.

Crewing for the same friend three times does not count.

Leave this room with a name and a date for phase 1. Students who leave it until week 14 cannot comply — there are not enough phases left, and component G is 10% of the project.
