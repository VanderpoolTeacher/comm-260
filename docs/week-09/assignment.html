Week 9 Assignment — 60-second piece and project file

COMM 260 · Introduction to Digital Media Production

Formative · submitted and critiqued · 0% of your course grade

Required before week 10's module summative. Accepted late without penalty until that summative is submitted.

Submitting late recovers the tools statement. It does not recover the lab observation — and this was the last of the three.

What to submit

  1. The finished 60-second piece, exported to the stated specification
  2. The project file, with its media
  3. Your tools statement — sequence settings and export choices, with reasons

All three. The exported video says nothing about how your media was organised, and outcome 9.2 is only assessable from the project.

What a complete answer looks like

A tools statement that would be marked Meets, in full:

Sequence — 1920×1080, 25fps. Matched to the footage, which I checked against what I wrote down in week 7 rather than trusting the preset. Our camera was on 25fps and the default the software offered was 30, so accepting it would have meant the software discarding and duplicating frames to make my footage fit a shape it was never shot in.

Media — everything copied into one project folder before I imported anything. Three bins: footage, audio, graphics. Our separate audio came off Priya's recorder and our footage off the team card, so they started in two places; I copied both in rather than linking, because the card goes back to the store cupboard at the end of the session.

Export — 1080p, 25fps, H.264, 10 Mbps, MP4. 1080p because the footage is 1080p and upscaling invents detail that was never recorded. 25fps to match, so no frames are being made up. H.264 in an MP4 because it plays everywhere without anyone installing anything, which matters because this is going onto a landing page in week 15. 10 Mbps because there is a lot of movement in the middle section and at anything lower it started breaking up in the shadows — I exported it twice to check.

What that demonstrates: every setting is attached to this footage and this piece. The media paragraph names where the files actually started, which is what makes it a real account rather than a description of good practice.

To reach Exceeds, name what a setting gave up. The bitrate answer is one clause away: "...which makes the file about twice the size, and I took that over the shadows falling apart."

How this is graded

Assessed on whether the export meets the stated specification. That part is checkable — it either does or it does not.

Plus your tools statement. Corroborated by lab observation. This is LO 2 evidence.

The reason is what distinguishes the work:

"1080p because the footage is 1080p and upscaling invents detail that is not there" — evidences competency

"1080p" — evidences following instructions

What does not count

  • A video without its project file. Outcome 9.2 cannot be assessed from an export.
  • A project that only opens on the machine it was made on. Media linked from a phone, a downloads folder, or a drive you unplugged.
  • Export settings copied from the specification with no reasons attached. That is the half of outcome 9.5 that is a precondition, not the outcome.
  • Cuts on action you found afterwards. You were asked to make two and point at them.
  • A piece longer than 60 seconds. The limit is where selection happens, and selection is the point.
  • Audio synced by eye and never listened to.

Rubric

LO 2 — Industry-standard tools

Demonstrate competency with industry-standard tools for digital media production, including cameras, audio equipment, and editing software.

Level
Exceeds Settings justified against the situation, and you say what the chosen setting gave up
Meets Operates the equipment unaided. Tools statement gives settings with reasons for this subject
Approaching Settings recorded accurately with no reasoning attached, or operation needed prompting
Not yet Defaults accepted; cannot say what was set or why

LO 4 — Video

Plan, shoot, and edit high-definition video using professional production techniques.

Level
Exceeds Departures are clearly decisions — you can say what the location turned out to be, and what you chose instead
Meets Plans, shoots, and edits. The piece is traceable to the plan and departures are named with reasons
Approaching Plan and piece do not obviously relate, or no departures are reported
Not yet No plan, or a plan written after the shoot — detectable because it contains no departures

The same four levels apply to every assignment in this course. Full descriptors and the whole map of which assignment evidences what: competency rubric.
