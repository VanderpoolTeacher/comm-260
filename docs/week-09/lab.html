Week 9 Lab — Edit the footage from week 8

COMM 260 · Introduction to Digital Media Production

This happens in the room. Bring your team's week 8 footage and the separately recorded audio, on storage you control. Bring closed-back headphones.

Open the files before you arrive. A student who discovers here that their storage will not mount has lost the session, and this is a lab observation week.

One thing here cannot be made up later. Your instructor watches you create a project and sequence unaided. This is the last of the course's three lab observations — week 5 was the recorder, week 7 the camera, and after today nobody watches you set anything up again.

What you are doing

Cutting your team's week 8 footage into a finished 60-second piece, on your own.

You shot it as a team. You edit it alone. Everyone on your team has the same material and will hand in a different piece. That is expected, and the difference between them is the most useful thing you will see today.

Part 1 · Create the project — this part is observed

Before anything else, and unaided:

  1. Create the project.
  2. Create a sequence matched to your footage — resolution and frame rate.

You will be asked what your footage is and how you know. If you wrote it down in week 7, this takes four seconds.

Do not accept a default preset without checking it. A preset is not wrong. An unchecked one is not a decision.

Part 2 · Organise the media — before you cut

This is the part everyone wants to skip, and its bill arrives in week 14 rather than today.

  • Put every piece of media in one place. Copy it in. Do not link to a phone, a downloads folder, or a drive you are going to unplug.
  • Name your bins for what is in them — footage, audio, graphics. Not Bin 1.
  • Keep the project file with its media.

Do this before you start cutting. Tidying up after an edit is not the same act — the project recorded where things were at the moment you imported them, and moving the media afterwards does not update that.

Part 3 · Sync the audio

Bring the separately recorded sound into the project and align it to the picture.

Verify on headphones. Not by eye. Speech that is a few frames out does not look wrong on a waveform. It sounds wrong immediately, and once you have heard it you cannot stop hearing it.

If your team recorded audio into the camera in week 8, tell your instructor now. There is material held for exactly this case.

Part 4 · Cut to 60 seconds

Sixty seconds. You shot more than that.

The limit is where the work is. Choosing what stays is the decision your week 8 shot list either helps with or does not.

At least two of your cuts must be cuts on action, and you must be able to point at them. A cut on action that happened by accident does not count — you are being asked to have made it.

Part 5 · Export to the specification

The specification is posted: resolution, frame rate, codec, bitrate, container.

Then write what each setting means. Not which value you used — what the setting does, and why that value for this piece. This is half of outcome 9.5, and it is the half people leave out.

Before you leave

What you hand in

See the assignment.

The 60-second piece, the project file, and your tools statement.

Both the video and the project. Outcome 9.2 is only assessable from the project — an exported file says nothing about how the media was organised.
