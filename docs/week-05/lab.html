Week 5 Lab — Record one subject three ways

COMM 260 · Introduction to Digital Media Production

Student-facing. Book your equipment before this lab. Bring closed-back headphones. Earbuds will not work.

What you are doing

Recording the same subject three ways — lavalier, shotgun, handheld — and comparing what each one does.

Your instructor watches each of you set up unaided. This is one of only three lab observations in the course.

Part 1 · Pair up

One records, one is the subject. Then swap. Everyone records three times and is recorded three times.

Part 2 · The script

You are given a 45–60 second script. Everyone uses the same one.

Identical words across all three setups is what makes the comparison legible. Different words would explain away every difference you hear.

Part 3 · Three setups, in order

For each of lavalier, shotgun, and handheld:

  1. Place the microphone. Position it — do not accept where it lands.
  2. Set gain so speech peaks between −12 and −6 dBFS. Watch the meter.
  3. Put headphones on and listen. Before you record, not after.
  4. Record a test. Listen back. Change something.
  5. Record the take.

Step 4 is the one people skip. A test you listened to and changed nothing about is not monitoring.

Part 4 · Room tone

30 seconds of the room saying nothing. Everyone silent, same space.

It will feel much longer than 30 seconds. Do the full 30.

You will be asked what it is for. You do not need the whole answer this week.

Before you leave

Listen back to all three recordings, on headphones, in the room.

A student who discovers a dead file at home has lost the week — and week 6 has nothing to edit.

What you hand in

See the week 5 assignment.

Three raw recordings and a written comparison.

Keep the files. Week 6 builds a piece from them, and week 6 requires the edited piece plus raw sources.

What quiet costs you

Recording too quiet feels safer than recording too hot. It is not.

Too hot Too quiet
Clipped. Obviously ruined. Sounds fine — until you lift it.
You know immediately. The noise floor comes up with the voice.

Both are recoverable in the ear. Neither is recoverable in the file.
