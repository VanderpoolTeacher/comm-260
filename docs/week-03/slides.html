Title

Week 3 — Shooting to intent

Week 2 taught you to see it. This week you make it.

2 / 14

Outcomes

By the end of this week you can

  1. Write a creative intent before you shoot
  2. Choose compositions that serve that stated intent
  3. Judge whether an image reads as intended to someone who did not write it
  4. Select and sequence a set so it reads as one body of work
  5. Emphasise your subject deliberately — at least two means in the same frame

3 / 14

This is a topic.

"I'm going to photograph the river."

It says what you will point at. It says nothing about what the pictures should do.

4 / 14

This cannot fail.

"To show emotion."

Any six images satisfy it.

An intent that cannot be missed cannot be served.

5 / 14

The test

What set of images would fail this intent?

If you cannot answer, rewrite it.

6 / 14

Rewrite one live

7 / 14

Composition is now a means

The question is no longer is this well composed.

It is does this composition serve what I said I was doing.

8 / 14

Emphasis you build

At least two of placement · scale · focus · contrast · light

in the same frame.

Shown in class.

9 / 14

Why two?

One can happen by accident.

Two, chosen together, is a decision.

10 / 14

Shoot 25. Submit 6.

You cannot select down from six.

11 / 14

Cutting is the work

A strong image that does not serve the intent

weakens the set.

12 / 14

The blind swap

Hand over six images only.

Your partner writes what they think it was meant to do.

Then you read your intent aloud.

13 / 14

The tools statement starts now

From this week, every project carries a tools statement

What you used · what you set · why those settings for that situation

"f/2.8 to drop the cluttered background" — competency

"f/2.8, 1/250, ISO 400" — record-keeping
