Week 3 Lab — Write an intent. Shoot to it.

COMM 260 · Introduction to Digital Media Production

Student-facing. Bring your camera, charged, with empty storage. Bring your draft intent in writing.

What you are doing

Writing a creative intent, shooting to it, then finding out whether a stranger can read that intent back out of your images without being told what it was.

Week 2 taught you to see composition. This week you make it — with a purpose attached.

Part 1 · Finalise and hand in your intent

Your intent is collected before you shoot. This is not a formality. An intent written afterwards always fits the images, because it was fitted to them.

A creative intent is one paragraph stating what the images are meant to do. Not what you will photograph — what the pictures should accomplish.

Test it: what set of images would fail this intent? If you cannot answer, it is too broad to serve.

Part 2 · Shoot

Shoot at least twenty-five frames. You are selecting down to six, and you cannot select from six.

On at least half your frames, build subject emphasis deliberately — using at least two of placement, scale, focus, contrast, and light in the same frame. Be able to say which two.

One means can happen by accident. Two, chosen together, is a decision.

Record your settings as you go. Aperture, shutter, ISO — and why, for the situations that mattered. That is your tools statement and it is much harder to reconstruct later.

Part 3 · Select down to six

Two questions per frame:

  1. Does it serve the intent?
  2. Does it repeat something another frame already does better?

A strong image that does not serve the intent weakens the set. Cutting it is the work.

Part 4 · The blind swap

Hand your six images only to a partner. Do not tell them your intent.

Your partner writes, in one or two sentences, what they think the images were meant to do.

Then you read your actual intent aloud.

The gap between the two is the point of the week. A small gap means the images carried it. A large gap means they did not — and that is useful information, not a failure.

Part 5 · Revise the selection

If your partner read something different, you can often fix it without reshooting — by changing which six images you chose.

That is what selecting down is for.

Before you leave

Keep the entire shoot. Not just the six.

Week 4 edits from this material and needs your originals intact. A later week in this course will also ask you for a photograph you made this term.

Delete nothing.

What you hand in

See the week 3 assignment.

Six images, your written intent, and your tools statement.
