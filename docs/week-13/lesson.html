Week 13 · Phase 2 — Capture — Lesson

COMM 260 · Introduction to Digital Media Production

Read this before the phase begins. About 15 minutes — this week is short on reading and long on production.

Three things before the phase starts:

  1. Write the creative intent for your photography set. Before shooting, as in week 3. Put it beside your style guide and check they are compatible — an intent that needs the environment legible and a guide specifying f/2.8 or wider is a contradiction, and you resolve it now rather than in the middle of a shoot.
  2. Book equipment twice. Photography and audio, one week. Check the calendar supports both before you assume it does.
  3. Confirm who you are crewing for this phase — and confirm they know.

1 · The compression, in numbers

Week 3 asked for This phase asks for
Photographs 6 12
Week 6 asked for
Audio 60 seconds 2–3 minutes

Plus crewing on somebody else's production.

This is the phase students underestimate more than any other, and the reason is structural: week 3 asked for photography and week 6 asked for audio, so nobody has ever had to do both at once. Now you do, at double the scale, in one week.

Plan the week before it starts. Both bookings, the crewing session, and the editing time.

2 · Nothing here is new

The photography is week 3. Creative intent written before capture, compositions that serve it, subject emphasis built deliberately, and a tools statement with reasons. At twelve images instead of six.

The audio is weeks 5 and 6. Mic chosen for the situation, gain in the −12 to −6 window, headphones on before recording, room tone captured. Then cut for clarity and for meaning, with a structural decision you can name. At two to three minutes instead of sixty seconds. Edited piece plus raw sources, exactly as week 6 required — which is precisely why week 6 required it.

If you are meeting something here for the first time, an earlier week did not do its job. Say so. That is a gap in the course, not a failure of yours, and it is worth naming now rather than discovering at the checkpoint.

3 · What is new: your own guide as a constraint

This is the first time you shoot against a specification — and you wrote it.

Outcome 13.1 is produce 12 photographs that meet your technical specs and serve a stated intent. Both halves, at once.

Week 3 rehearsed one half. You wrote an intent and made images that served it. Nobody was checking those images against a written technical standard, because there wasn't one.

Now there is, and it is yours.

Week 3 Week 13
Intent, and images that serve it Intent, and images that serve it — and comply with your own written values
Judged on whether the intent was served Judged on both, and you can pass one and fail the other

A set can be technically perfect against your guide and serve no intent. A set can serve its intent beautifully and ignore every value you wrote. Neither is the outcome.

4 · When your guide and your intent contradict

At some point this week, they will.

Your guide says shallow depth, f/2.8 or wider, every portrait. Your intent says the work happens in the room, and the room has to be legible. Those cannot both hold.

This is not a mistake. It is what specifications are for — they surface conflicts you would otherwise resolve unconsciously, one frame at a time, and never notice.

Two legitimate resolutions:

Revise the guide Revise the intent
"Shallow for portraits, f/8 or narrower where the environment is the subject." "The portraits are close and the environment is established in two wide frames."
You discovered your rule was too broad You discovered your intent needed narrowing

And one illegitimate one: ignore whichever is inconvenient and hope nobody checks.

Revising your style guide is allowed. Revising it silently is not.

Record what you changed and why. Do not backdate it — a guide quietly rewritten after the shoot to match what you happened to capture is week 3's intent-after-shooting problem, wearing a different document.

Resolve it before you shoot, not during. The lab's first part is for exactly this.

5 · Two things about the audio that get abandoned under pressure

This is the most common failure of this phase, and it is not a skill failure — it is a time failure.

Recorded at the last minute, in a bad room. Week 5's outcomes are entirely intact in your head and they get abandoned when there are two days left. A room that rings ruins a recording that no editing recovers, and by week 13 you know that.

Raw sources missing. Week 6 required the edited piece and the raw. So does this. Without them the claim that your edit improved on the source is not checkable — and unassessable is a worse outcome than imperfect.

Book the quiet space early. It is bookable and it runs out.

6 · Crewing is part of this week, not an errand

Outcome 13.3. One session this phase, on a classmate's production, in a support role — and the producer signs your log.

Students schedule this last and treat it as a favour they are doing. Component G is 10% of the project — the same weight as your entire photography set.

Get the signature at the time. A crew log signed retroactively in week 15 is not evidence of anything, and it will be treated accordingly.

And check the constraint: three sessions across weeks 12–14, three different producers. If phase 1 was for the same person you are about to crew for again, change one of them now — there is only one phase left after this.

Module 5 vocabulary

No new terms this week. These are the ones that matter here, all defined in the course glossary:

Term Means
Photography set The images made for the landing page, submitted with capture settings and creative intent. Component B
Audio piece The edited audio made for the landing page, submitted with raw sources. Component C
Creative intent One paragraph written before shooting, stating what the images are meant to do
Raw sources Unedited original recordings, kept and submitted
Crewing Working on a classmate's shoot in a support role
Crew log The record of it, signed by the producer you crewed for
Disclosure log Per-submission record of source material and AI assistance

Two bars worth re-reading. Photography set is barred from being called a photo essay — and raw sources is barred from originals, masters, source files, and unedited.

Practise

Run guide versus intent before the phase begins. Spot the contradictions between a guide and an intent, choose which one to revise, and tell a legitimate revision from a backdated one.

Round 1 is the one to do properly. Finding the conflict before you shoot is the difference between one revision and twelve unusable frames.

Check yourself before the phase

  1. How many photographs, and how many minutes of audio? Compare both to weeks 3 and 6.
  2. What are the two halves of outcome 13.1, and can you pass one without the other?
  3. Your guide and your intent contradict. Name your two legitimate options and the illegitimate one.
  4. What makes a style guide revision legitimate rather than backdated?
  5. What must be submitted alongside the audio piece, and why?
  6. How many different producers must appear across your three crew log entries?

If you cannot answer 4, re-read section 4. It is the distinction this checkpoint watches for.

What happens in the phase

You resolve your guide-against-intent conflict in the room, then produce both components on your own time. Checkpoint 2 checks the photography on both halves at once, and the audio against its own raw sources.

Full detail in the lab sheet and checkpoint 2.

Thanksgiving falls in week 14, which loses two sessions. If you can pull any of your video planning forward into this phase, do it — next phase is the one that will feel the squeeze.
