Checkpoint 2 — Photography and audio

COMM 260 · Introduction to Digital Media Production

Formative checkpoint · project rubric, components B and C

Component Of the project Of the course
B · Photography set 10% 4%
C · Audio piece 10% 4%

What to submit

  1. Photography set — 12 images, with capture settings and your creative intent
  2. Audio piece — 2–3 minutes, with your raw sources
  3. Crew log entry, signed by the producer you crewed for
  4. Any style guide revision, recorded and dated, with the old version kept
  5. Disclosure log, updated

Plus, in the room: critique across all three dimensions. Component H, at every checkpoint.

How this is graded

Assessed on photography on both halves at once, audio against its own raw sources.

Both halves at once means outcome 13.1's two clauses are checked together: does the set meet your technical specs, and does it serve your stated intent? A set can pass one and fail the other, and both are named in the assessment.

Against its own raw sources means the audio's claim — that the edit is better than the raw — is checkable rather than asserted. That is week 6's principle, unchanged and for the same reason.

Your work is checked against your guide

Not against taste, and not against whether the guide was a good idea.

The guide was assessed at checkpoint 1. This checkpoint assesses whether the media obey it.

That cuts both ways. A set that complies with a modest guide passes on compliance. A set that ignores an ambitious guide does not — however good the images are.

What a complete answer looks like

The part students get wrong is the tools statement paired with the intent. Here is one that would be marked Meets:

Creative intent (written before shooting). The set is about how much of a maker's day is spent on things that are not making — waiting, checking, tidying, deciding. Twelve images, all in one workshop, and no more than three of them show anything actually being made.

Style guide compliance. Available light throughout, nothing above 5600K, saturation down roughly 20% in post. Subject on a third in every frame; no centred horizons.

Revision, dated before the shoot. My guide originally said f/2.8 or wider on every portrait. That collided with the intent — if the set is about the room, the room has to be legible. I changed it to f/2.8 or wider for close portraits, f/8 or narrower where the environment is the subject. What it cost: the guide is more permissive now, so two images can look quite different and both comply. I accepted that to keep the intent.

Capture settings. f/8, 1/125, ISO 400 for the eight environment frames — deep enough that the bench and the far wall both read, and 1/125 because the light was falling and I would rather have the ISO than the blur. f/2.8, 1/250, ISO 200 for the four close portraits.

Why those, for this subject. The environment frames are the argument — waiting happens somewhere, and a shallow frame would have deleted the somewhere. The four close frames are the exception my revised guide names, and they are where the day gets personal.

What that demonstrates: the intent came first and constrained the shoot. The revision is dated, reasoned, and its cost is named. And the settings are attached to this subject rather than listed.

To reach Exceeds, name what the set as a whole gave up. This is one clause away: "twelve frames in one workshop means the set reads as one body of work, and it also means I have no evidence this is true of anyone but him."

How the checkpoint runs

1 Critique in the room, all three dimensions — component H
2 Photography checked on both halves at once — your specs and your intent
3 Compliance checked against your own guide, not against taste
4 Audio checked against its own raw sources
5 Crew log verified — date, whose shoot, role, what you were responsible for, what went wrong, and the producer's signature
6 Phase 3 crewing confirmed booked, with a different producer

What does not count

  • Twelve images that are week 3's six, twice. Selecting down happens once, across the whole set. Outcome 3.4's reads as one body of work still applies.
  • A style guide quietly ignored. Your images are held against your own written values.
  • A guide revised silently after shooting. Legitimate to revise. Not legitimate to backdate. Keep the old version.
  • Intent written after shooting. Week 3's problem, at higher stakes, and detectable the same way — an intent written afterwards describes what was captured rather than constraining it.
  • Audio recorded at the last minute in a bad room. A room that rings ruins a recording no editing recovers.
  • Missing raw sources. Despite week 6. Without them the audio cannot be assessed as specified.
  • A crew log signed retroactively, or a third session with a producer you have already crewed for.

Use the glossary

This week: Photography set, Audio piece, Creative intent, Capture settings, Raw sources, Tools statement, Crewing, Crew log, Disclosure log, Checkpoint.

Two bars worth re-reading. Photography set is barred from photo essay, image set, gallery, and photoshoot. Raw sources is barred from originals, masters, source files, and unedited — as a noun for the files.

Rubric

LO 3 — Photography

Capture high-definition photography that reflects technical accuracy and creative vision.

Level
Exceeds Both halves met, and the images function as one body of work rather than individually competent frames
Meets Meets stated technical specifications and demonstrably serves the stated creative intent
Approaching One half only — technically clean with no discernible intent, or expressive but unusably exposed or focused
Not yet Neither half — or the intent was written after the shoot to fit what was captured

LO 5 — Audio

Record and edit audio with attention to clarity, quality, and storytelling impact.

Level
Exceeds A structural decision changes how the piece lands, and you can say what it cost
Meets Audibly better than the raw — clear, and shaped by a structural decision. Raw sources submitted
Approaching Cleanly cut, but says what the raw said. Nothing structural happened
Not yet Processed speech, noise reduction as the main edit, or no raw sources

LO 9 — Collaboration

Collaborate effectively in production teams, demonstrating communication and project management skills.

Level
Exceeds The producer day shows sequencing decisions — what ran in parallel, what changed when something failed
Meets Held a defined role and the producer role. The team can say what you were responsible for
Approaching Present and contributing, but accountability is unclear or was never held
Not yet Teammates cannot say what you were responsible for

On LO 9 here: crewing evidences the collaboration half only. The project management half is evidenced in week 8 and nowhere else — crewing puts you on someone else's production without ever making you accountable for a shared result.

The same four levels apply to every assignment in this course. Full descriptors and the whole map of which assignment evidences what: competency rubric.
