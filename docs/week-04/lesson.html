Week 4 · Editing, and the first critique — Lesson

COMM 260 · Introduction to Digital Media Production

Read this before the session. About 20 minutes.

Bring your week 3 images — the full shoot, originals intact. Bring your week 3 intent.

Read this first

You hand in an edited set. You are graded on the critique.

That is not a trick. It is the widest gap between submitted and assessed anywhere in this course, and every year people submit a beautiful edit with three sentences of praise attached and are surprised by the grade.

1 · Editing is a decision, not a repair

Ask most people what editing is for and they say fix — brighten the dark ones, straighten the crooked ones, rescue the mistakes.

That is a small part of it, and it is not what this week assesses.

A correctly exposed photograph can still be edited — to make it mean something it did not mean before. Warmer and closer. Cooler and more clinical. Same file, two different pictures, neither of them a rescue.

Outcome 4.2 says it exactly: correct exposure and colour to serve intent, not only to rescue error.

Go back to the intent you wrote in week 3. A set meant to feel close and warm and a set meant to feel clinical get different treatments from the same raw material. For each adjustment you make, you should be able to say which part of your intent it serves.

2 · Non-destructive, and why

Keep the original intact. Work on a copy, an adjustment layer, or whatever your software's non-destructive equivalent is.

The reason is not caution. It is this:

An edit you cannot undo is a decision you cannot revisit.

And this course grades revisiting. Every week asks you to make a choice, look at the result, and change your mind if the result says so. An overwritten original removes that option permanently.

It is also the only way one file goes two directions — which is the demonstration you will see in the session.

3 · Critique — the larger half of this week

The three dimensions. Every time.

Dimension Asking
Technical quality Is it correctly made? Exposure, focus, colour, sharpness.
Aesthetic design Do the compositional choices work? Does anything have emphasis?
Narrative effect Does it say what it is trying to say?

Left alone, people give one — usually aesthetic, phrased as a preference — and stop. Requiring all three is what turns critique from a reaction into a structure.

These three recur at all four checkpoints of the final project. This week is where they are taught.

4 · Preference, or judgment?

This is outcome 4.4 and it is the same move you learned in week 1, applied to someone else's work.

Preference Judgment
"I don't like the crop" "The crop cuts the hand out, and the hand was the only thing telling us she was working"
"The colours are too much" "The saturation lifts the background to compete with her face, so the eye goes to the wall"
"It feels a bit flat" "Everything is lit evenly, so nothing has emphasis — your eye has nowhere to land"

The judgment names what was lost and why it mattered. The preference names how you felt.

You are allowed preferences. You are not allowed to submit them as critique.

5 · "Specific enough to act on"

The test:

Does the person receiving it know what to do next?

Fails Passes
"The lighting could be better" "The light is behind her, so her face is the darkest thing in a bright frame — a reflector, or moving ninety degrees, would fix it"
"Nice composition" "The subject sits centre with even light, so nothing pulls the eye — emphasis is doing no work here"
"It's a strong image" "The set says it's about labour but every frame is a portrait — nothing shows the work itself"

Praise is not critique. It is pleasant, it is often true, and it gives the person nothing.

6 · Being critiqued

Stay silent while your work is being discussed. You may respond after all three dimensions have been given.

You will want to explain the circumstances — the light was bad, you only had ten minutes, the subject moved. All of that may be true and none of it changes what is on the wall.

Explaining is how a critique gets deflected, and you cannot hear it while you are defending it.

Module 2 vocabulary — additions

Term Means
Critique Structured spoken assessment of other people's work across technical quality, aesthetic design, and narrative effectiveness
Non-destructive Editing that leaves the original file unchanged

The glossary bars feedback, crit, peer review, and discussion as substitutes for critique — they are looser words for a thing that has a fixed structure here.

Check yourself before the session

  1. What are you actually graded on this week?
  2. Name the three critique dimensions.
  3. Why is non-destructive editing a requirement rather than a suggestion?
  4. Rewrite "I don't like the crop" as a judgment.
  5. What is the test for whether a critique is specific enough?
  6. Why do you stay silent while your own work is critiqued?

If you cannot answer 1, read the top of this page again.

Practise

Run preference or judgment before the session — sort real critique sentences, then repair the weak ones. Unlimited attempts, no marks.

What happens in the session

You edit, then you critique work that is not yours, on the projector, across all three dimensions. Full detail in the lab sheet and the assignment.
