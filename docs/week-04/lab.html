Week 4 Lab — Editing, and the first critique

COMM 260 · Introduction to Digital Media Production

Student-facing. Bring your week 3 images — the full shoot, originals intact, not only the six. Bring your week 3 intent.

What you are doing

Editing your week 3 set, then giving your first graded critique of someone else's work.

Read this twice: the edited set is what you hand in. The critique is what is graded.

Part 1 · Set up non-destructively

Before you edit anything, make sure your originals are untouched and you are working on a copy, an adjustment layer, or your software's non-destructive equivalent.

This is checked at the start of the lab. It is easy to verify now and impossible to recover later.

The reason is not caution. An edit you cannot undo is a decision you cannot revisit — and revisiting is what this course grades.

Part 2 · Edit toward your intent

Re-read the intent you wrote in week 3. Edit toward it.

For each adjustment, be able to say which part of your intent it serves.

Editing is not rescue. A correctly exposed image can still be edited to mean something it did not mean before — and the strongest work in this module comes from students doing exactly that.

Produce a before/after for each image in your set.

Part 3 · Critique

Your work goes on the projector. So does everyone's.

Pairings are assigned. You are not critiquing a friend.

For the work you are given, write and then speak one specific, actionable point per dimension:

Dimension Asking
Technical quality Is it correctly made? Exposure, focus, colour, sharpness.
Aesthetic design Do the compositional choices work? Does anything have emphasis?
Narrative effect Does it say what it is trying to say?

All three, every time.

Part 4 · Being critiqued

Stay silent while your work is being critiqued. You may respond only after all three dimensions have been given.

You will want to explain the circumstances. Explaining is how a critique gets deflected, and you cannot hear it while you are defending it.

What you hand in

See the week 4 assignment.

Your edited set with before/after, and your written critique.

The test your critique has to pass

Does the person receiving it know what to do next?

This fails This passes
"The lighting could be better" "The light is behind her, so her face is the darkest thing in a bright frame — a reflector, or moving ninety degrees, would fix it"
"Nice composition" "The subject sits centre with even light, so nothing pulls the eye — emphasis is doing no work here"
"It's a strong image" "The set says it's about labour but every frame is a portrait — nothing shows the work itself"

The left column is a preference. The right column is a judgment: it names what was lost and why it mattered.
