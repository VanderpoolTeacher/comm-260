Week 4 Assignment — Edited set, and your first graded critique

COMM 260 · Introduction to Digital Media Production

Module summative · 10% of your course grade

What to submit

Two distinct things.

  1. Your edited set, with before/after for each image — originals intact
  2. Your written critique of the work you were assigned

Read this before you start

The edited set is the deliverable. The critique is what is assessed.

You can submit a beautiful edit and fail this assignment on a vague critique. This is the widest gap between what you hand in and what is judged anywhere in this course, and it is stated here so that it cannot surprise you.

How this is graded

Assessed on your critique of someone else's work — all three dimensions, specific enough to act on.

The test: does the person receiving it know what to do next?

Your edited set demonstrates outcomes 4.1 and 4.2 and must be complete. It is not what carries the grade.

The three dimensions

Dimension Asking
Technical quality Is it correctly made?
Aesthetic design Do the compositional choices work?
Narrative effect Does it say what it is trying to say?

All three. Every time. These recur at all four checkpoints of the final project — this is where they are taught.

What does not count

  • Praise. "This is really strong work." Not actionable, satisfies no dimension.
  • Three dimensions saying one thing. Noticing an image is dark technically, aesthetically, and narratively is one observation, not three.
  • Preference presented as judgment. "I don't like the crop" is a preference. "The crop cuts the hand out, and the hand was the only thing telling us she was working" is a judgment.
  • A destructive edit. If your original is gone, outcome 4.1 cannot be evidenced.
  • An edit that only rescues. Correction serves intent. It is not only repair.

If you missed week 3

You must have shot your own set before this lab.

If you arrive without images you can still complete the critique — 4.3 and 4.4 are the graded outcomes and do not require your own work — but you cannot demonstrate 4.1 or 4.2, and the assignment is incomplete.

Use the glossary

Check your words against the glossary before you submit.

This week: Critique — which fixes the three dimensions and bars feedback, crit, peer review, and discussion as substitutes.

Rubric

LO 8 — Critical evaluation

Critically evaluate media projects for technical quality, aesthetic design, and narrative effectiveness.

Level
Exceeds Distinguishes preference from what the work does not achieve, and names what was lost
Meets All three dimensions, one specific and actionable point each
Approaching Warm, general and true — or three dimensions all making the same observation
Not yet Praise. Not actionable, satisfies no dimension

LO 3 — Photography

Capture high-definition photography that reflects technical accuracy and creative vision.

Level
Exceeds Both halves met, and the images function as one body of work
Meets Meets stated technical specifications and demonstrably serves the stated creative intent
Approaching One half only — technically clean with no discernible intent, or expressive but unusably exposed or focused
Not yet Neither half — or the intent was written after the shoot to fit what was captured

The same four levels apply to every assignment in this course. Full descriptors and the whole map of which assignment evidences what: competency rubric.
