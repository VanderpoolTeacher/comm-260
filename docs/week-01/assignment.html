Week 1 · Assignment — Three choices

COMM 260 · Introduction to Digital Media Production

Module summative · 5% of your course grade

This is graded. Week 1 is not a warm-up — it is the module summative for Module 1, and the only place in this course where naming a choice is assessed on its own.

Done on your own, after the lab.

What to submit

Three choices you noticed — one from each piece. One from the photo essay, one from the audio piece, one from the short film. Not three from whichever was easiest.

For each, write:

  1. The choice, named in course vocabulary
  2. What it accomplishes
  3. What the obvious alternative would have been, and what it would have cost

Roughly 100–150 words per choice. Credit the piece you are writing about.

What a complete answer looks like

Choice. The photographer shoots the whole essay at eye level except the third image, which is from above.

What it accomplishes. For nine images you are standing beside these people. In the tenth you are looking down on the room, and you suddenly see how few of them there are. The shift does the work a caption would otherwise have to do.

The alternative, and its cost. Staying at eye level throughout would have kept the intimacy unbroken — but the scale of the room would never have registered, and the essay would be about individuals instead of about how many are missing.

Note what the third part does. It does not say the alternative would have been worse. It says what it would have bought and what it would have lost.

What does not count

  • Preference. "The colour grading is really nice." That is what you think, not what the maker did.
  • Description without a name. "It's really quiet in the middle" is true and is not a choice.
  • Three choices from one piece. One per piece.
  • Hedging. "I think maybe the director possibly wanted…" Make the claim.
  • Naming only the upside. Every choice costs something. Find it.

A note on being wrong

You cannot know what the maker was thinking, and you are not assessed on guessing correctly.

You are assessed on whether you can tell a deliberate choice from a default nobody questioned — and on whether you can say what it would have taken to do otherwise.

A confident, specific, wrong reading scores better than a vague, cautious, correct one.

Use the glossary

You get the glossary this week and it does not change for the rest of the term. Check your words against it — especially the Never use column.

Rubric

LO 1 — Aesthetic and narrative principles

Apply aesthetic and narrative principles to create compelling digital media projects.

Level
Exceeds Names the choice, what it accomplishes, and what the alternative would have cost
Meets Names the choice in course vocabulary and says what it accomplishes
Approaching Describes the work accurately but does not name the choice — or names the technique without saying what it does
Not yet States a preference. "It looked better."

What this means for your three choices

"The shallow depth isolates him from the crowd" — not "the background is blurry."

The gap between Meets and Exceeds is the third part of your answer. Naming what a choice accomplished gets you Meets. Naming what it cost is what moves it.

That is the same line in every week of this course, and it is outcome 1.4 — state what a given choice accomplishes, and what the alternative would have cost.

Vocabulary accuracy matters, but function matters more. Naming a technique slightly wrong while clearly identifying what it does sits above the right term with no account of what it accomplishes.

The same four levels apply to every assignment in this course. Full descriptors and the whole map of which assignment evidences what: competency rubric.
