Week 1 · Lab — Reading three pieces

COMM 260 · Introduction to Digital Media Production

This happens in the room, with your instructor. It is not graded.

Bring nothing. Everything you need is provided. Read the lesson first.

Lab or assignment?

Students mix these up every year, so:

This lab The assignment
Where In the room On your own, afterwards
Graded No Yes — 5% of your course grade
Purpose Build the skill out loud, where being wrong is free Evidence the skill, unaided

This is where you can be wrong cheaply. Use it for that.

What you are doing

Reading three professional pieces — one photo essay, one audio piece, one short film — and naming the choices you see in each.

You are not making anything this week. You do not need a camera.

Part 1 · The photo essay

Work alone and in silence first.

Write down five choices you notice. Use whatever words you have — you are not expected to have the vocabulary yet.

Then we take your list apart together and rewrite it, on the board, in course vocabulary. This is the point of the session. Your sentences, improved.

Look for:

  • What is in the frame — and what was left out
  • Where your eye goes first, and what put it there
  • Whether the subject is sharp and the background is not
  • What is repeated across images

Part 2 · The audio piece

First listen: screens down, nothing in your hands. Just listen. Nobody hears structure on a first listen.

Second listen: take notes.

Look for:

  • Where the speech is, and where it is not
  • Whether music is under the speech, over it, or absent
  • The gaps. How long they are and where they fall

You will be asked what you did not mention. It is almost always the silence — and then you will hear the demo, which is the same passage with the pauses removed.

Part 3 · The short film

First viewing: what happens.

Second viewing: how it was made to happen.

Look for:

  • How close the camera is to people, and whether that changes
  • What the sound is doing that the picture is not
  • What you were made to notice, and what you were kept from noticing

Before you leave

Every person in the room says one named choice out loud.

Not a preference — a choice, with what it accomplishes. It does not have to be clever.

Not optional, not graded. It is here because writing a description is easy to do by accident and saying a name out loud is not.

Then, on your own

See the assignment — three choices, one per piece. That one is graded.
