Building a Media Producer's Presence — Presentation Rubric

COMM 260 · Step 3c of the Course Build Process

Scored independently of the work presented. 15% of the project.

Evidences: LO 1 — Apply aesthetic and narrative principles to create compelling digital media projects.

Format: 5–7 minutes, plus questions. The live landing page on screen — not slides about it.

Why this is separate

Making the work and accounting for the work are different competencies. Averaging them lets a polished presentation carry weak media, and lets a strong maker who cannot explain their decisions pass an outcome they have not met.

A student may score high here and low on the project rubric, or the reverse. Both are meaningful results, not errors to be reconciled.

This rubric does not re-assess the deliverables. Whether a photograph is well exposed is scored on the project rubric. Whether the student can say why they exposed it that way is scored here.

1. Naming the choices

Level Descriptor
Exceeds Choices are named precisely in discipline vocabulary, and the student distinguishes decisions they made from conventions they inherited.
Meets Aesthetic and narrative choices are named using discipline vocabulary rather than described in general terms.
Approaching Choices are described but not named — "I made it darker" rather than an account of what the exposure decision was doing.
Not yet Describes what was made without identifying choices at all. A walkthrough of deliverables is not an account of decisions.

2. Defending against alternatives

Level Descriptor
Exceeds The rejected alternatives are real contenders, and the student can say what would have made them the better choice.
Meets Choices are defended against specific alternatives that were considered and rejected, with reasons tied to the stated positioning.
Approaching Alternatives mentioned but not weighed, or the rejected option is one no one would have chosen — a defence that costs nothing.
Not yet No alternatives. "It looked better" is preference, not defence.

3. Holding up under questioning

Level Descriptor
Exceeds Extends the reasoning into cases not prepared for, and concedes accurately where a question exposes a genuine weakness.
Meets Answers why not the other way? about decisions not covered in the prepared talk, consistently with what was presented.
Approaching Handles prepared ground but repeats the talk when pressed beyond it.
Not yet Cannot answer beyond the script, or answers contradict the prepared account.

4. Coherence of the account

Level Descriptor
Exceeds The account explains the presence as a system — why these three pieces, in these forms, for this producer.
Meets The identity, the photography, audio, and video pieces, and the landing page are presented as one connected set of decisions rather than four separate reports.
Approaching Each piece is accounted for individually; the connections between them are asserted rather than explained.
Not yet A sequence of unrelated walkthroughs.

Questions

Questioning is assessment, not a formality. Allow roughly as long for it as for the talk.

The standard question is "why not the other way?" — asked about a decision the student did not choose to discuss. This is what criterion 3 measures, and it is the only part of this assessment that distinguishes reasoning the student holds from reasoning they rehearsed.

Ask about at least one decision from each deliverable. A student who prepared thoroughly will have anticipated some of them; that is a good outcome, not a failure of the question.

What is deliberately not scored

Eye contact, pacing, slide design, and speaking confidence are not assessed. No learning outcome in this course concerns presentation delivery, and scoring it would be preference dressed as assessment.

Clarity is assessed only where it prevents the account from being followed — and then it is scored under criterion 4, as coherence, not as polish.
