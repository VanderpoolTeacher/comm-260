COMM 260 — Competency Rubric

The master rubric for every assignment in the course.

Ten competencies, one per course learning outcome, four levels each. Every weekly assignment cites from here rather than inventing its own criteria.

Outcomes are reproduced verbatim from the course specification. Terms follow the Glossary.

How to use it

An assignment does not assess all ten. Most assess one. Week 4 assesses two.

Each assignment states which competencies it evidences and what the week's specific evidence is. The level descriptors below do not change week to week — that is the point of a competency rubric. What changes is the artifact the student produces.

Level Means
Exceeds The competency is demonstrated, and the student can account for what the choice cost
Meets The competency is demonstrated
Approaching Half of it is demonstrated, or it is demonstrated without being accounted for
Not yet Not demonstrated

Same four levels as the project rubric and the presentation rubric, so a student meets one scheme across fifteen weeks.

The line between Meets and Exceeds

Almost always the same line: Meets names what a choice accomplished. Exceeds also names what it cost.

That is not a stylistic preference. It is outcome 1.4 — state what a given choice accomplishes, and what the alternative would have cost — generalised across the course, and it is the distinction the whole course is built to teach.

A note on wording

These descriptors are written in plainer language than the outcomes they assess. They never say anything different. Where a descriptor and its outcome appear to diverge, the outcome is correct and the descriptor is a defect — report it rather than teaching to the rubric.

LO 1 — Aesthetic and narrative principles

Apply aesthetic and narrative principles to create compelling digital media projects.

The competency: making choices that serve a purpose, and being able to name them.

Level
Exceeds Names the choice, what it accomplishes, and what the alternative would have cost
Meets Names the choice in course vocabulary and says what it accomplishes
Approaching Describes the work accurately but does not name the choice — or names the technique without saying what it does
Not yet States a preference. "It looked better."

LO 2 — Industry-standard tools

Demonstrate competency with industry-standard tools for digital media production, including cameras, audio equipment, and editing software.

The competency: operating the equipment unaided, and saying why those settings for that situation.

Level
Exceeds Settings justified against the situation, and the student says what the chosen setting gave up
Meets Operates the equipment unaided. Tools statement gives settings with reasons for this subject
Approaching Settings recorded accurately with no reasoning attached, or operation needed prompting
Not yet Defaults accepted; cannot say what was set or why

Evidenced by the tools statement on every project from week 2 onward, corroborated by lab observation in weeks 5, 7, and 9. "f/2.8 to drop the cluttered background" is competency. "f/2.8, 1/250, ISO 400" is record-keeping.

LO 3 — Photography

Capture high-definition photography that reflects technical accuracy and creative vision.

The competency: both halves at once — technically sound and serving a stated intent.

Level
Exceeds Both halves met, and the images function as one body of work rather than individually competent frames
Meets Meets stated technical specifications and demonstrably serves the stated creative intent
Approaching One half only — technically clean with no discernible intent, or expressive but unusably exposed or focused
Not yet Neither half — or the intent was written after the shoot to fit what was captured

LO 4 — Video

Plan, shoot, and edit high-definition video using professional production techniques.

The competency: the finished piece is traceable to the plan, departures included.

Level
Exceeds Departures are clearly decisions — the student can say what the location or subject turned out to be, and what they chose instead
Meets Plans, shoots, and edits. The piece is traceable to the plan and departures are named with reasons
Approaching Plan and piece do not obviously relate, or no departures are reported
Not yet No plan, or a plan written after the shoot — detectable because it contains no departures

LO 5 — Audio

Record and edit audio with attention to clarity, quality, and storytelling impact.

The competency: the edit is demonstrably better than the raw, and both are submitted so it is checkable.

Level
Exceeds A structural decision changes how the piece lands, and the student can say what it cost
Meets Audibly better than the raw — clear, and shaped by a structural decision. Raw sources submitted
Approaching Cleanly cut, but says what the raw said. Nothing structural happened
Not yet Processed speech, noise reduction as the main edit, or no raw sources — which makes it unassessable

LO 6 — Linear and non-linear

Differentiate between linear and non-linear storytelling methods and apply them appropriately in media projects.

The competency: naming what each structure costs, not only what it gains.

Level
Exceeds Defends the chosen structure against the rejected alternative on its own terms
Meets Distinguishes the two, structures material both ways, and names a genuine cost for each
Approaching Names gains only — or names a "cost" that is a gain restated
Not yet Conflates non-linear with interactive, or produces the linear version with links added

LO 7 — Integration

Integrate multiple media forms (photography, video, audio) into cohesive digital productions.

The competency: the piece survives the removal test.

Level
Exceeds The division of labour is deliberate, and the analysis says specifically what would break
Meets Each medium carries something the others cannot. Removing any one breaks the piece
Approaching All media present, but removing one changes nothing — assembled, not integrated
Not yet Two media saying the same thing, or a third that is decorative

A correctly diagnosed failure outranks an untested claim. A student whose piece does not survive but who identifies precisely why has demonstrated the harder half of this competency, and is graded accordingly.

LO 8 — Critical evaluation

Critically evaluate media projects for technical quality, aesthetic design, and narrative effectiveness.

The competency: a critique the recipient can act on.

Level
Exceeds Distinguishes preference from what the work does not achieve, and names what was lost
Meets All three dimensions, one specific and actionable point each
Approaching Warm, general and true — or three dimensions all making the same observation
Not yet Praise. Not actionable, satisfies no dimension

The test: does the person receiving it know what to do next?

LO 9 — Collaboration

Collaborate effectively in production teams, demonstrating communication and project management skills.

The competency: being accountable for a shared outcome, not merely present at one.

Level
Exceeds The producer day shows sequencing decisions — what ran in parallel, what changed when something failed
Meets Held a defined role and the producer role. The team can say what this student was responsible for
Approaching Present and contributing, but accountability is unclear or was never held
Not yet Teammates cannot say what this student was responsible for

The team's account is what makes an individual grade possible from shared work. A student's own reflection does not evidence this on its own.

LO 10 — Original professional content

Produce original digital media content suitable for a variety of professional career paths and contexts.

The competency: original work, positioned in a specific professional context.

Level
Exceeds The work visibly follows the conventions the positioning claims
Meets Work is original, and positioning names a specific professional context and the conventions it follows
Approaching Original, but the context is generic — "content for people"
Not yet Not original, or no stated context

Two rules this rubric does not break

Criteria never restate an outcome differently. Descriptors are plainer than the outcomes; they never say something else. If they diverge, the outcome is right.

A rubric level is not a percentage. These are competency levels. How they map to the weights in the course outline is a grading decision and lives there, not here.
