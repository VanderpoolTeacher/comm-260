COMM 260 — Course Glossary

The controlled vocabulary for this course. Every artifact — student brief, rubrics, instructor guide, slides, LMS pages, assignment titles — uses these terms and only these terms.

How to use it: before any content ships, check every term in it against this list. If a word appears in the Never use column, replace it. If a needed term is missing, add it here first, then write the content.

One term, one meaning, everywhere. A student who learns a word in week 1 must find that same word in week 12.

How this document is organised

One glossary for the course, sorted into two kinds of term.

Part Holds Who it is for
1 · Course-wide Structure, assessment, and what students hand in alongside every project. Used in all fifteen weeks Everyone, every week
2 · Module vocabulary The teaching vocabulary, grouped by the module that owns it The week that teaches it
3 · Terms retired Words that were wrong and must not come back Anyone editing older drafts

A module owns a term. It is introduced there, assessed there, and used freely everywhere after. A week that needs a word another module owns names the owning module rather than redefining it — this is what stops week 7 teaching week 9's vocabulary as though it were its own.

Where a term is introduced in one week and assessed in another, both are stated. Contrast is the standing example: mentioned in passing in week 1, owned and assessed in week 2.

Part 1 · Course-wide

Course structure

Term Means Never use
Phase One of the four week-long blocks inside the final project: Phase 1–4. A module is a block of the course; a phase is a block of the project. stage, unit, week block, sprint
Checkpoint The end of a phase, where deliverables are submitted and critique happens. Four of them. milestone, due date, gate, deadline, review
Formative An assessment whose purpose is feedback. Submitted and critiqued; carries 0% of the course grade. Every weekly activity is formative. Still required — it is the raw material the module summatives are built from. practice, ungraded, low-stakes, homework, optional, extra credit
Formative requirement The rule that formative work must be submitted before that module's module summative. Accepted late without penalty until the module summative is submitted; not accepted after. Carries no penalty of its own — the module summative is scored on what was submitted. gate, prerequisite, eligibility rule, hard stop, lockout
Module summative The assessment at the end of a module, judging whether the module's outcomes were reached. Weeks 1, 4, 6, 8, 10, 11 — six of them. Carries the weight. unit summative, test, exam, unit project, big assignment
Course summative The final project. 40% of the course grade. final exam, capstone, culminating assessment
Knowledge check A written test of concepts and terminology. One in the course, week 10. quiz, exam, test (alone), assessment
Practical A module summative where the student makes or runs something rather than writing about it. Weeks 4, 6, 8, 11. performance task, hands-on, application
Module One of five blocks of weeks sharing a subject. Ends in a module summative. unit, section, block, chapter
Component One of the nine graded parts of the project, A–I. Used in the rubric and design documents. element, section, part, piece
Deliverable A specific thing a student submits or performs. Student-facing word for a component's output. artifact, asset, submission, item
Final project The whole four-week summative, weeks 12–15. capstone, final, project (alone), summative (with students)
Survey Material named and explained so the vocabulary exists, but not performed and not assessed. Week 2's camera mechanics. overview, intro, background, FYI
Found-image collection Week 2's deliverable — published images gathered to demonstrate compositional terms. Students photograph nothing. moodboard, inspiration board, scrapbook

Assessment

Term Means Never use
Lab observation Instructor watching a student operate equipment unaided during a lab. Corroborates the tools statement where the two disagree. Weeks 5, 7, 9. equipment check-off, tool check, gear check, skills test, practical
Team production The week 8 shoot planned and run by a group of three with rotating roles. The only shared-accountability work in the course. group project, collaboration exercise, group work
Crewing Working on a classmate's shoot in a support role. Weeks 12–14. helping, assisting, collaborating, volunteering
Critique Structured spoken assessment of other people's work across technical quality, aesthetic design, and narrative effectiveness. Component H. feedback, crit, peer review, discussion
Presentation The live 5–7 minute defence plus questions, walking the landing page. Component I, scored on its own rubric. final presentation, defense, pitch, talk, showcase
Project rubric Scores components A–H. 85%. main rubric, assignment rubric, grading rubric
Presentation rubric Scores component I. 15%. oral rubric, speaking rubric
Container A component that is required and graded but evidences no learning outcome. Currently only the brand foundation. scaffold, support, non-graded, filler

What students hand in alongside

Term Means Never use
Tools statement Submitted with every project: the equipment and software used, the settings chosen, and why those settings for that situation. The evidence for LO 2. gear list, equipment list, tech notes, specs sheet
Capture settings Aperture, shutter, ISO, white balance recorded at the time of shooting. Part of the tools statement, not a substitute for it. metadata, EXIF, camera settings, specs
Creative intent One paragraph written before shooting, stating what the images are meant to do. concept, vision, artist intent, mood
Raw sources Unedited original recordings, kept and submitted. originals, masters, source files, unedited (as the noun for the files. The adjective stands — "five shots, unedited, in intended order" is week 7's deliverable, fixed by the course outline)
Planning documents Shot list, treatment, or storyboard — made before the shoot. pre-production, prep, plan (alone), paperwork (as a name for the documents. Permitted as the name of the failure — planning documents made to satisfy a requirement and never opened again have become paperwork. Week 8's topic line is fixed by the course outline as "planning as thinking, not paperwork")
Project file The editing application's session file. timeline, session, edit file, working file
Disclosure log Per-submission record of source material and AI assistance. AI statement, citation, attribution, credits
Student file A file issued to students to build on or fill in — a code scaffold, a fillable document, a worksheet. Distinct from the lab sheet (what to do) and the assignment (what to submit). Lives in the week that uses it. starter, starter file, handout, resource, boilerplate, template (reserved for process templates in 02_design/templates/)
Crew log Running record of work done on classmates' shoots, signed by the producer crewed for. collaboration log, teamwork record, peer log

What the final project is made of

Module 5's vocabulary. Named from week 1 onward so students know what the course is building toward, defined here once.

Term Means Never use
Landing page The published HTML page that presents the whole body of work. It is the integrated piece — photography, video, and audio brought together in one place. Component E. launch piece, website, site, portfolio, homepage, web page
Presence The producer identity the landing page represents. A concept, not a file. Use landing page whenever referring to the thing being built or graded. brand presence, online presence, profile
Brand foundation Logo, brand guide, and style guide together. Component A. identity, branding, brand package
Brand guide States positioning, audience, voice, and what the student makes. brand book, brand doc, positioning doc
Style guide The specific visual and sonic rules the media must follow — hex codes, typefaces, aperture and lighting rules, tempo and pacing limits. visual guide, style sheet, look book
Photography set The images made for the landing page, submitted with capture settings and creative intent. Component B. photo essay (as a name for the student's own set. Permitted for published work students analyse — week 1's lab reads a professional photo essay, and calling that a "photography set" would imply it is somebody's component B), image set, gallery, photoshoot
Audio piece The edited audio made for the landing page, submitted with raw sources. Component C. podcast, audio story, sound piece, recording
Video piece The edited video made for the landing page, submitted with planning documents and project file. Component D. film, reel, video story, movie
Positioning statement Names who the work is for and the conventions it follows. 300–500 words. Component F. artist statement, reflection, rationale, about page

Part 2 · Module vocabulary

Module 1 · Visual literacy — week 1

The analytical vocabulary. Introduced in week 1, used for the remaining fourteen.

Term Means Never use
Preference A statement about what you like. Says nothing about what the maker did, and is not evidence about the work. opinion, taste, gut feeling, vibe
Named choice A statement of what the maker did, in course vocabulary, together with what it accomplished. The unit of analysis in this course. observation, comment, note, read
The frame The edge of the image, and therefore everything included and everything excluded. shot (alone), composition (alone), border
Exclusion What was deliberately left outside the frame. A decision — and the one students never notice, because absence draws no attention to itself. cropping out, leaving out, omission
Deliberate choice Something the maker decided, where doing otherwise was genuinely available to them. intentional, on purpose, artistic choice
Default Something the maker never questioned, or could not have changed. The alternative to a deliberate choice — not a mistake. accident, mistake, laziness, error
Cost What a choice gave up. Every choice has one, and naming it is what separates a judgment from an opinion. downside, drawback, trade-off, sacrifice
Pace How long an audience is held on one thing before being moved to the next. Applies to picture and to sound. timing, rhythm, speed, tempo
Silence Sound deliberately withheld. It does work — weight, space, emphasis — and it is invisible because nothing is there. Distinct from room tone (module 3), which is a recording of a room rather than a choice. dead air, gap, nothing, empty space

Module 2 · Photography — weeks 2–4

Introduced in week 2, applied in week 3, assessed in week 4.

Term Means Never use
Contrast A difference the eye or ear reads first — bright against dim, sharp against soft, sound against silence. One of the five means of subject emphasis. Introduced in passing in week 1; owned and assessed in week 2. difference (alone), pop, stands out, opposition
Subject emphasis What makes the subject read first — placement, scale, focus, contrast, or light. Introduced week 2, applied week 3. focal point, emphasis (alone), subject isolation, pop, hero the subject
Subject placement Where the subject sits in the frame. One of the means of subject emphasis, not a synonym for it. positioning, composition (alone), rule of thirds
Balance How visual weight is distributed across the frame. symmetry, evenness, harmony, equilibrium
Negative space What is deliberately left empty inside the frame. Distinct from exclusion, which is outside it entirely. empty space, white space, dead space, background
Leading the eye The route attention travels through the frame. leading lines, guiding the viewer, eye flow, visual path
Framing within the frame Something in the scene used as a border. natural frame, internal frame, doorway shot
Cropping Deciding the edge in the camera, at the time of shooting. reframing, trimming, cutting (reserved for the edit)
Shallow depth of field A narrow band of the image is sharp. Isolates the subject, and loses context. blurry background, bokeh, f-stop blur
Deep depth of field Everything is sharp. Keeps context, and emphasises nothing on its own. everything in focus, wide focus, all sharp
Frozen motion The moment stopped — movement rendered sharp. fast shutter (that is the setting, not the effect), action shot
Motion blur Movement conveyed, detail lost. blur (alone), slow shutter (the setting, not the effect), camera shake

Module 3 · Audio — weeks 5–6

Introduced and observed in week 5, assessed in week 6.

Term Means Never use
Gain How much the recorder amplifies what the microphone hears. volume, level (alone), input, sensitivity
dBFS The scale the meter uses. 0 is the ceiling; everything usable is below it. decibels (alone), dB, volume units
Clipping Signal exceeding 0 dBFS. Distorted, and unfixable. peaking, distortion (alone), overloading, red-lining
Noise floor The constant low-level sound underneath everything. Lifting a quiet recording lifts this too. hiss, background noise, static
Room tone Thirty seconds of a room with nobody speaking. Specific to that room, that day. Distinct from silence (module 1), which is a choice rather than a recording. ambience, atmos, background, dead air
Phantom power Power sent to a microphone that needs it. Either on, or the microphone is silent. 48v (alone), mic power, power
Monitoring Listening on headphones while recording, to catch problems as they happen. Not the same as listening back afterwards. checking, listening back, playback
Lavalier Microphone worn on the body. Close, consistent, stays out of frame. lav (in student-facing text), clip mic, tie mic
Shotgun Directional microphone. Rejects sound from the sides; picks up what it is pointed at. boom (that is the pole), directional mic, rifle mic
Handheld Microphone held in the hand. Robust, forgiving, easy to move. stick mic, reporter mic

Module 4 · Video and integrated production — weeks 7–11

The heaviest module. Five weeks, three module summatives, and the vocabulary the final project runs on.

The camera — week 7

Term Means Never use
Frame rate How many images the camera records per second. fps (alone, in prose), speed, frames
Shutter How long each individual image is exposed. Governs how motion looks within the frame. exposure (alone), shutter time
Resolution How many pixels make up each image. quality, size, definition, HD (alone)
Shot size How much of the world the frame contains — wide through close-up. shot type, framing (alone), distance
Coverage Shooting the same moment at more than one size, so the edit has options and the change between sizes carries meaning. b-roll, extra shots, options
Sequence A run of shots that reads as one continuous piece of action. Outcome 7.3 is shoot a sequence that reads without dialogue. See the note below — week 9 uses this word for something else. scene, montage, string of shots

Planning and production — week 8

Term Means Never use
Treatment Short prose stating what the piece is, who it is for, and what it is trying to do. Commits to something, and therefore rules things out. synopsis, outline, pitch, concept
Shot list Every shot intended, in a shootable order, derived from the treatment. shot plan, list (alone), schedule
Storyboard Drawn shots, for the sequence whose relationships are hard to hold in the head. Outcome 8.2 is a sequence, not the production. boards, sketches, animatic
Production roles Four accountabilities, not job titles. Director owns what the shot is · Camera owns the image · Sound owns the recording · Producer owns the day. jobs, positions, crew roles (reserved for crewing)
Producer The role that owns the day — the schedule, the equipment, whether the thing finishes. Producing is deciding what happens next. The only place in the course a student is accountable for other people's time, and the sole evidence for LO 9. organiser, manager, team lead, coordinator

The edit — weeks 9–11

Term Means Never use
Sync Aligning separately recorded audio to picture. Week 9's outcome 9.3, and the reason week 8 records audio separately. syncing up, matching, lining up
Cutting on action Cutting mid-movement so the action carries across the join. match cut, action cut, hiding the cut
Media management Organising footage so the project can be reopened later and still work. file management, housekeeping, organisation
Export specification The stated format, resolution, and settings a finished piece must be delivered in. render settings, output, export (alone)
Linear Told in one fixed order, start to finish. sequential, chronological, traditional
Non-linear The audience chooses the path through it. interactive, branching, random access, modular
Removal test Delete one medium from the landing page. If nothing breaks, the media were assembled rather than integrated. integration test, cohesion check
Integrated The media are interdependent — removing one breaks the piece. combined, mixed, multimedia, blended
Assembled Media merely co-present. The failing state the removal test detects. layered, stacked, thrown together

⚠ Unresolved — sequence carries two meanings

Week 7 uses sequence for a run of shots that reads as one continuous piece of action — outcome 7.3, shoot a sequence that reads without dialogue.

Week 9 uses sequence for the editing application's timeline — outcome 9.1, create a project and sequence that match your footage.

Both are fixed by the course outline, and one word carrying two meanings is exactly what this glossary exists to prevent.

Week 7's meaning is defined above, because it is taught first and outcome 7.3 is built on it. Week 9's is the collision, and it is the one that needs a decision — before week 9 is written.

Option Cost
Call week 9's the timeline in all student-facing material The editing software on screen says sequence. The course would disagree with the tool students are looking at
Keep both, disambiguated every time — shot sequence / timeline sequence Two qualified terms where the course elsewhere insists on one unqualified one
Let week 9 own sequence and rename week 7's to something else Week 7 is already built and outcome 7.3 says sequence. This is the expensive one

Michael's call. Nothing downstream is blocked by it except week 9's own vocabulary table.

Module 5 · Final project — weeks 12–15

Module 5 introduces no new craft vocabulary. It is where every earlier module's terms are used at once.

Its nouns — landing page, brand foundation, photography set, audio piece, video piece, positioning statement — are defined in Part 1 under What the final project is made of, because students meet them from week 1 and they are not a module's teaching vocabulary. Phase, Checkpoint, Component, Crewing, Crew log, Critique, and Presentation are also in Part 1.

This section exists to say that, so its absence does not read as an omission.
