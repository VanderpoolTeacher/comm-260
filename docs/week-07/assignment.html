Week 7 Assignment — Five shots that read

COMM 260 · Introduction to Digital Media Production

Formative · submitted and critiqued · 0% of your course grade

Required before week 8's module summative. Accepted late without penalty until that summative is submitted.

Read this carefully: submitting late recovers the tools statement. It does not recover the lab observation. Week 7 is the only place in this course where a video camera is observed, and there is no later week that replaces it.

What to submit

  1. Five shots, unedited, in intended order
  2. Your tools statement — frame rate, shutter, resolution, and why each value

What a complete answer looks like

This is a tools statement that would be marked Meets, in full:

Frame rate — 25fps. The subject is a person making coffee, so there is nothing fast in the sequence and nothing that needed slowing down afterwards. I picked the frame rate the room was standardised on so my footage will cut against everyone else's in week 9, and because nothing in the shoot gave me a reason to depart from it.

Shutter — 1/50. I wanted the movement to look ordinary rather than draw attention to itself. Roughly double the frame rate is what reads as ordinary, and ordinary was the point — the sequence is about a small domestic task and any stylisation in the motion would have been the loudest thing in it.

Resolution — 1080p. The storage I booked would not hold the whole shoot at anything higher, and I would rather have five usable shots at 1080 than three at 4K. I have written it down so my week 9 sequence can match it.

What that demonstrates: each value is attached to this subject and this shoot. The convention is named in the shutter answer, but it is doing work in the sentence — it explains the decision rather than standing in for one. And the resolution answer names a constraint and what was chosen over what.

To reach Exceeds, add what each setting cost. The 1/50 answer is one clause away: "...which means I have no ability to hold on a single frame if I want a still out of this, and I accepted that."

How this is graded

Assessed on whether the five shots read as one sequence with no dialogue and no titles to carry them.

Plus your tools statement. Corroborated by lab observation. This is LO 2 evidence.

Two separate things are being judged here and they do not rescue each other. A sequence that reads beautifully with a tools statement of three numbers evidences one of them. So does a careful tools statement attached to five shots nobody can follow.

What does not count

  • A sequence you have to explain. If it needs narrating, it did not read — and the explanation is the evidence.
  • Five shots of the same size. That is one long take chopped up.
  • Camera movement that reveals nothing. Say what arrived at the end of the move.
  • Settings with no reasons. "1/50, 25fps, 1080p" is a list.
  • A rule offered as a reason. "Because it's the standard" — whose, and for what?
  • An edited sequence. Unedited, in intended order.

Use the glossary

This week: Tools statement, Lab observation, Capture settings, Deliberate choice, Default, Cost.

The glossary bars gear list, equipment list, and tech notes as substitutes for tools statement, and it bars gear check and skills test for lab observation.

Rubric

LO 2 — Industry-standard tools

Demonstrate competency with industry-standard tools for digital media production, including cameras, audio equipment, and editing software.

Level
Exceeds Settings justified against the situation, and you say what the chosen setting gave up
Meets Operates the equipment unaided. Tools statement gives settings with reasons for this subject
Approaching Settings recorded accurately with no reasoning attached, or operation needed prompting
Not yet Defaults accepted; cannot say what was set or why

LO 4 — Video

Plan, shoot, and edit high-definition video using professional production techniques.

Level
Exceeds Departures are clearly decisions — you can say what the location turned out to be, and what you chose instead
Meets Plans, shoots, and edits. The piece is traceable to the plan and departures are named with reasons
Approaching Plan and piece do not obviously relate, or no departures are reported
Not yet No plan, or a plan written after the shoot — detectable because it contains no departures

The same four levels apply to every assignment in this course. Full descriptors and the whole map of which assignment evidences what: competency rubric.
