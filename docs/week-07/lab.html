Week 7 Lab — Five-shot sequence

COMM 260 · Introduction to Digital Media Production

This happens in the room. Bring the camera and tripod you booked, charged, with empty storage.

Lab or assignment?

This lab The assignment
Where In the room, with your instructor On your own, afterwards
Graded No Formative — 0%, and required
Purpose Shoot a sequence where being wrong is free Evidence that it reads, unaided

One thing here cannot be made up later. Your instructor watches you configure the camera, and that observation happens only in this room, only today. Submitting the assignment late gets you the tools statement back. It does not get you the observation, and no later week in this course observes a video camera.

What you are doing

Shooting a five-shot sequence with no dialogue that reads on its own.

You are not editing it. You are handing in exactly what came out of the camera, in the order you meant it to be seen — because the question is whether the shots were planned to read, not whether an edit rescued them. Decide that before you shoot, not after.

Part 1 · Configure — this part is observed

Set, on your own camera, unaided:

  • Frame rate
  • Shutter
  • Resolution

You will be asked why each value. Out loud. Three times.

A rule A reason
"Because it's the standard." "Because I want the motion to look natural rather than stuttered."
"Because that's what it was on." "Because the subject moves fast and I want each frame sharp enough to hold on."

A rule will be sent back for a reason. "Because it's the standard" is not a wrong answer — it is not yet an answer.

This happens before anyone shoots anything. Do not start filming while you wait.

Part 2 · Take a subject

Choose from the provided list. Each one is a small task with a clear beginning and an end — making coffee, unlocking and opening a door, writing and posting a note.

Do not invent your own. You will spend the lab on the idea instead of on the shots, and the shots are the whole assessment.

Part 3 · Shoot five shots

Five shots. No dialogue. No titles. The sequence has to communicate what happened using picture alone.

Requirements:

  • At least three different shot sizes among the five
  • At least three of the five on the tripod
  • Shoot in your intended order, and keep that order

The three-size requirement is there because five shots at one size is not a sequence — it is one long take chopped up, and there is no grammar in it to read.

The tripod requirement is there because you cannot decide between a locked-off shot and a moving one if you have never done the first.

Part 4 · Screening

Some sequences are played to the room with the sound off.

The room says what happened. If they cannot tell, it did not read — and you find that out here, where it costs nothing, rather than at submission.

If your sequence is played, do not explain it. Let it run. Whatever you would have said is the thing the sequence was supposed to say.

Before you leave

Check that all five shots are on the card, and that they play.

A student who discovers a dead file at home has lost the week's deliverable, and there is no reshoot session.

What you hand in

See the assignment.

Five shots, unedited, in intended order — plus your tools statement.

Why there is no auto mode

In week 2, manual mode, white balance, and ISO were surveyed — named and explained so the words existed, and explicitly not assessed.

This is the week that ends. Video gives you no auto mode to hide in, which is precisely why the survey happened five weeks before you needed it.
